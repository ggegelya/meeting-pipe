## The short answer

No. A recorder that sits outside the meeting client cannot be 100 percent reliable, and none of the competitors is. Four reasons that no engineering inside this app removes. (1) macOS has no public statement that a call is live, or which browser tab holds the microphone, so every outside recorder infers. (2) The best inference there is, 'this app's process is capturing microphone input', is close to necessary but not sufficient: a listen-only or audio-off join never raises it, some clients keep the microphone after a call, and some may drop it on mute or hold. (3) Vendors change their clients without notice. On this laptop one Teams window-title change on 2026-10-08 produced four chopped recordings and about 300 prompts that flashed and vanished (inherited from this run's field check). (4) Consent is a human step unless you remove it, and that step was the largest measured loss here: all 32 prompt timeouts in the pack ran with default action skip, 30 of them Teams, and at 29 of those 30 the last microphone reading for Teams was true (re-derived). Detection was right and the call was still lost. The competitors, as read in this run's research (inherited, not re-fetched): Recall.ai, the one vendor that publishes a detection changelog, shipped fixes for missed, phantom, early-closed and never-closed meetings through 2026, including Meet in a background tab; Granola documents that back-to-back calls can merge and that pressing Stop is the most reliable end; MacWhisper labels its detection beta and recommends recording critical meetings by hand; the open-source trackers (OpenOats, pasrom/meeting-transcriber, screenpipe) carry reports of Meet in Chrome not being picked up and of sessions that flap or never close. They feel reliable for two reasons this app can copy: nearly all of them trigger on a known app's process holding the microphone (browsers included), and they prompt or ask 'keep recording?' instead of acting when unsure. What is honestly attainable on this laptop: for native Teams, which is 66 of the 74 recordings, a detector whose errors are rare, visible and cheap. That means every call that opens the microphone is offered or recorded within seconds, no recording is stopped while Teams still holds the microphone, the transcript is cut at the microphone release, and an unsure state is shown as unsure with one button. A late end costs a trimmed tail, a wrong end costs a linked second part, and nothing is lost silently. Zoom should follow the same rules but has 3 calls of evidence. For Google Meet in a browser the honest answer today is 'unknown until one scripted probe call': the pack holds 0 microphone readings for any browser, so I can promise a prompt when the Meet tab is visible (a title fix) and nothing more until the probe says whether Chrome's audio process can be read through a call.

## Why this class keeps recurring

- (browser-start) Start detection for a browser is a string match on a third-party page title read through a channel that depends on UI state (selected tab, on screen, current Space). It is a proxy for the fact of interest, which is that a call is using the microphone, and the proxy is neither necessary nor sufficient.
- (browser-start) False positives are loud and misses are silent, so every incident so far has narrowed the pattern (2026-06-30, END5) and nothing pushed back. There is no row for a refused Meet-branded tab, no heartbeat, and the one miss auditor is fed by an input that has never read true, so zero reads as clean.
- (browser-start) There is no ground truth for browsers. The title fixture has no browser rows, all Meet tests use one synthetic code-shaped title, and the corpus Meet scenarios are synthetic seeds. Tests pin the implementation's assumption about Meet, not Meet.
- (browser-start) Two gates in series with different oracles and AND semantics (Accessibility title at scan time, ScreenCaptureKit on-screen title at engage), plus a hidden third permission. Each new gate was added to remove a false start; none has a fallback when it cannot see.
- (browser-start) A level fact is sampled as instants by a poll, and suppression state (cooldown, skip latch, resume window) is keyed on the browser's bundle id because no meeting identity exists. Start is decided once, early, and a wrong or missed early decision persists for the meeting.
- (browser-start) Start and end use opposite philosophies for the same weak signal: once recording, a tab title flip is held for up to 12 minutes; before recording, the same flip ends the meeting and removes the prompt immediately.
- (browser-start) Probe-first with owner-owed probes and no deadline freezes known-broken paths: DET10 (2026-08-15) and DET6 (2026-08-18) have waited three months, and the one signal that demonstrably reads true in this log (per-process audio) is fenced off from browsers pending those measurements.
- (browser-start) The repo and the running app diverge: fixes land at HEAD while the installed binary is from 2026-08-18, so field evidence never tests the current code and current code is never tested by the field.
- (native-start) Presence is inferred by scraping another vendor's UI. The start bar is made of window titles and control labels read through Accessibility at a 3 to 12 s poll; that surface hides, renames, localizes and re-renders at the vendor's pleasure, and each incident has been answered with one more rule on the same oracle.
- (native-start) A level fact is rebuilt from samples and timers. 'Is this call still up' is answered as 'did a poll see a confident UI state in the last 60 s', so every consumer (skip latch, cooldown, candidate grace, resume window) carries its own grace constant, each sized on one app's measured blackout and wrong for the next app.
- (native-start) There is no meeting identity. Skip, cooldown and resume are keyed by bundle id and wall clock, in memory. The system cannot tell the call I skipped from the next call in the same app, or a false end from a real one followed by a new call, so it alternates between re-asking and recording unasked.
- (native-start) The one signal that is app-attributed, permission-free and indifferent to UI state (per-process HAL input) has been removed, revived for ends, and re-added to starts as a poll-time tiebreaker; it is still not the thing that opens or keeps a call. The device-level mic flag chosen for the catch-all has never fired and went unmeasured for four months.
- (native-start) Start and end share one gate. Discovery only acts when the state is idle, so every late, held or missed end is also a missed start for the next call, and every end fix (holds, resume) silently changes start behaviour.
- (native-start) Consent is a 30 s modal moment with loss as the default and no pre-roll. Reliability of the start therefore rests on a person reacting during the busiest seconds of a meeting.
- (native-start) Tuned and tested on Teams. The corpus holds dozens of Teams end traces, two synthetic Zoom ones and no start-side trace at all; scorer tests use hand-written tuples. Zoom's config comment says it rides on the resolver rather than on a measurement. Nothing replays 'toolbar hidden for two minutes'.
- (native-start) Decisions are not reconstructible. A suppressed sighting on the latch path logs nothing, an unchanged drop set and an empty one both log nothing, scan rows on the installed build carry no audio reading, the build identity is not in the log, and 960 doctor probe rows from three development days sit in the same file. Each fix has had to be validated by the next real meeting.
- (native-start) Fixes are written faster than they are installed or verified. Twelve weeks of detection work is not running on this Mac, and most of END9 to END17 plus five DET tasks sit at partial waiting on owner probes, so the owner keeps meeting defects that are closed on paper.
- (false-end) Change-only edges feeding a level decision. Every signal dedupes against its last value and emits only transitions, and the engine reconstructs 'what is true now' from the last edge it happened to receive. A healthy button, a present window and a live mic are silent, so the engine cannot be reminded of them (END8's revert that never arrives, a hold nothing can release), and it cannot tell 'still live' from 'last said live an hour ago' (the frozen veto).
- (false-end) First readings and stale stamps count as evidence. lastValue starts nil, so the first sample is delivered as an edge even when it is 'ended'; depending on a three-queue race it is dropped forever, ends .starting directly, or opens a hold. Stamps record when a state was first seen and are later read as how long something has been true, across hours and across unrelated holds (the pair dwell, the lone dwell under a veto, the trim point).
- (false-end) No 'unknown'. A signal that cannot read (TCC nil, a vanished HAL object, a blank accessibility tree, an unreadable log) holds its prior value, so absence of information is indistinguishable from confirmation of the last state. END10, END12, END13 and END16 are each this in a different place.
- (false-end) Absence of UI is the end evidence. Window not in the on-screen list, title not matching, Leave element invalid: each has many causes other than the call ending and all are controlled by third-party rendering. The only positive call-state readings (the app holding the mic, audio still arriving from the far side) are used as a veto for two native apps or as a 15 minute ceiling, and not at all for browsers, where the owner's Meet calls live.
- (false-end) The decision is spread over four clocks that do not share assumptions: the signal (dedupe, throttled re-walk), the engine (phases, dwells), the host hold (first stamp, teardown consult, trim) and the session state machine (cooldown, skip latch, resume), plus the idle backstop beside them. Each END fix changed one layer and assumed the neighbour followed: END8 assumed the signal emits, END9 assumed the watcher runs, END11 assumed an adapter exists, END14's remembered stamp became END17's pair partner, END17's lone dwell composes with END13's veto into an instant end.
- (false-end) Irreversible actions hang off the weakest evidence. Installed: stop on one sample. HEAD: the stop is late, but the cut is anchored at the first weak signal and destroys audio under regulated or NDA, so the design still loses the middle of a meeting on the same class of false reading.
- (false-end) Verification is per layer with hand-injected events, on a corpus that is Teams-only. Engine tests feed events production never emits; the lifecycle traces are few and none ends a long live hold; Zoom is unmeasured by any probe and browsers have no trace of a mid-call tab change. Fixes ship green without running (the repo's own gotchas say so for END9, END10, END11).
- (false-end) The field loop is open. The app on this Mac contains none of END9 to END17 and nothing in the product or the log says which build produced an event, so two months of fixes were never exercised here and the owner's reports describe rules that were already rewritten.
- (missed-end) The end of a call is inferred from the absence of third-party UI artefacts (a button, a window title, the frontmost tab's title). None of them is under the app's control, each can vanish while the call is live, and each can persist after it is over. Only Teams has anything better, and that is a private log file.
- (missed-end) There is no positive, continuously sampled 'this call is up' reading for most surfaces. The one that exists (per-process mic capture) covers two native apps, is edge-only, and is excluded from browsers, which is where the owner's Meet calls happen.
- (missed-end) Signals are edges without freshness. The engine stores a last-known state and a first-seen timestamp, so an unreadable signal is indistinguishable from an unchanged one, and a stuck reading has unlimited authority (the veto, the stale pair stamp, the browser title that holds prior on nil).
- (missed-end) One decision carries three questions: is the call over, when did it end, and may the file be cut. The hold answers the first by waiting, then answers the second with the timestamp of the first doubt, and the third follows automatically. Recording long made the first safe and moved the unrecoverable error into the cut.
- (missed-end) Meeting identity is the app's bundle id and pid. The Meet code, the Teams call id and the Zoom meeting are never carried, so 'the same call came back' and 'the next call started' are the same event to the hold, the resume window, the skip latch and the cooldown.
- (missed-end) The backstop depends on the state it is supposed to back up: it stands down while the lifecycle reads live, is reset by unrelated sound, and is fed through a deduped stream. There is no unconditional ceiling anywhere on the path.
- (missed-end) Definitive evidence is gated behind weak evidence: the teardown line is read only after an AX or window artefact opens a hold, and a user Stop, the strongest signal of all, bypasses the trim logic entirely.
- (missed-end) Each field incident produced a rule (END2, END5, END6, END8, END9 to END17), each rule traded early ends against late ends for one shape, and all of END9 to END17 are still 'partial' because none was confirmed in the field. The build on this Mac predates every one of them, so the owner's daily experience and the code under discussion have been three months apart.
- (missed-end) The event log cannot answer 'how did this recording end and was that right'. A manual stop writes no event, there is no single end-decision row naming the route, and there is no ground truth, so rates cannot be measured and regressions surface only as owner complaints.
- (missed-end) Tests exercise the pure engine with hand-injected events that production does not emit (G006, G025) and synthetic traces. The composition that actually fails (signal, adapter, engine, host hold, trim, post-processor) has no end-to-end test, and no trace contains a genuine end after a long hold.
- (mic-and-mute) The question 'who is on the microphone' is never asked of the system that knows. Each mic fact is inferred through a proxy tied to 'the default input device' as it was at one of four different moments (daemon launch, recording start, the last default-input change, the last engine reconfiguration), while the call itself may be on another device.

## Recommended target design

BASE AND WHY. The merged design is the mic-truth-first proposal with grafts from the other two. All three designs share one spine (an always-on per-process microphone monitor, consent bound to a call session, the cut at the microphone release, an age gate on Teams mute log lines) and both critics picked mic-truth-first because its end state is the smallest: one monitor and one pure reducer that a single reviewer can hold. I rejected the fusion engine's window-title hashes and contested close (they put vendor strings back into the end path) and the capture-first design's audio before consent, shelf and vault (they conflict with ADR 0016 and the repo root/PRODUCT.md on a work laptop). Whether Teams itself moves onto the reducer is gated on field numbers after the patches (see the plan); browsers use it as soon as their probe passes, because their current path has one weak end signal and nothing worth keeping. There is one authority per app family at any time, never two. The evidence for the spine, re-derived on the pack: the per-process flag was the reading that was right. In the newest build all 4 detected Teams ends were led by the microphone release (inherited classification: three real ends and one real 98.7 s drop); 7 of 69 native recordings were stopped with no final release before the stop, 4 in era A and 3 in era B (inherited identification: the four chopped October recordings and three hand stops); across eras the stop followed the final release by a median 4.8 s (A, n=18), 5.5 s (B, n=38) and 62.2 s (C, n=6). Its limits: every microphone row in the pack was logged while the old detector was already looking, so how often an app holds the microphone with no call is unmeasured, and 495 rows came from the initial read (421), the poll (70) and the list listener (4), none from the per-object listener, so the poll is the only clock.

SIGNALS. (1) MicHolderMonitor, primary, new in MeetingPipeCore beside the repo root/daemon/Sources/MeetingPipeCore/Lifecycle/Signals/ProcessAudioSignal.swift and reusing its enumerate, match and read code behind CoreAudioHALBus. It runs from daemon start in every app state at a fixed 1 Hz with no backoff (today's signal backs off to 5 s, which blurs a release stamp by up to 5 s). Each second it reads kAudioHardwarePropertyProcessObjectList, and per object the bundle id, pid and kAudioProcessPropertyIsRunningInput (IsRunningOutput and the device list are logged only). Objects map to an app family by the union of dotted bundle prefix, pid ancestry and, for WebKit only and only if the owner allows a private call, the responsible process; the longest registry id wins so Chrome and Chrome Canary do not merge. Per family it publishes a level with a read time: held true, false or unknown. Unknown means the list or every matched read failed, the sample is older than 3 s, or a holding object vanished less than 5 s ago. The list listener only requests a coalesced refresh, at most one per second, and never logs per callback (inherited from the critiques: one burst of 7,637 unresolved rows in 4.9 s on 2026-10-12). The daemon's own pid is excluded. No TCC grant is needed (measured by DET8; inherited). (2) App exit of the family's main process: the one definitive automatic end, for native apps and browsers alike. A helper exiting is only a release. (3) Sleep and wake: stop at willSleep with the mark at sleep time; after a wake, levels are unknown until re-sampled and every clock restarts. (4) The existing scanner and scorer (Accessibility titles and call controls): they name the meeting, route it to a workflow, act as an offer-only second opener, and serve as the independent witness in the weekly report. They never end anything. (5) The recorder's own two channel levels: the unconditional ceilings and the far-side check. The microphone side reads level, never the mute verdict. (6) Owner actions (Record, Skip, Stop, Still in call, Finish now, Not a meeting): definitive in every state, each logged with a cause. (7) Mute sources (Accessibility mute control, Teams log lines, system mute): metadata only. Not used as evidence anywhere: AVCaptureDevice.isInUseByAnotherApplication (0 mic_busy rows of any kind in 114,553 events), device-level running reads during a recording, HAL voice activity (unsupported at every start, inherited), the calendar (ADR 0011), and the Teams teardown line in the end path (2 confirms in the whole pack; at the newest build's 4 Teams ends the Teams log had last been written 129 to 1,390 s earlier, so the line was not on disk in time).

STATE MACHINE. MicSession.decide(state, event, now) is a pure reducer in Core that returns the next state and a list of effects (show or hide offer, start recorder, stop recorder with cause and cut, log). Events are monitor samples, owner actions, app exit, sleep and wake, and recorder callbacks. There is one state per family and at most one recording, bound to at most one session. idle: family held true moves to arming. arming: held false returns to idle with nothing shown; unknown pauses the clock; held true for the family's open debounce D opens a Session {id, family, pids, openedAt, consent undecided}. Then, in order: if another family is recording, track silently and offer when that recording ends; if a hand-started recording with no session is running and this is the only open session, bind it; if a recording of this family closed less than R ago, apply the rejoin rule below; if the family is set to always record, start; otherwise offered. offered: the offer exists for as long as the session does. After prompt_timeout_sec the panel collapses to a menu-bar line with a Record row; default_prompt_action may be record or byo (start at the timeout, only if the family still reads held true) or keep (stay collapsed). It can no longer be skip. Record at any time starts the recorder. Skip or Not a meeting moves to declined. declined: nothing is asked again while the session is open, and a session of the same family that reopens within R of the close inherits the decline. A hand Record still works and binds to the session. recording: held true stays. Unknown for more than 10 s raises unsure condition 1 and changes nothing else. Owner Stop stops now with cause user_stop and marks the session declined so the still-held microphone does not offer again. releasing: entered from offered, declined or recording on the first held false sample, with releasedAt set to that sample. While recording, the HUD changes at once to 'Call ended, finishing in 1:00' with Still in call and Finish now, and the provisional cut is releasedAt. Held true again returns to the previous state, clears the mark and logs mic_gap with the gap length. Unknown pauses the clock. Still in call returns to recording with an owner hold that suspends automatic closing until the microphone has returned, the owner stops, or a ceiling. A different family opening its own session while this one is releasing finalizes this one now (superseded). Held false for the close grace G in total, or Finish now, closes. close: stop the recorder with cause mic_release and cut at releasedAt plus 2 s, hide any offer, go to idle. A queued session that opened while the recorder was still stopping is offered or started when the stop completes. app exit from any state closes at once with the cut at the last true sample. ceilings while recording, none of which consults a lifecycle verdict or a mute state: both channels below level for the configured idle horizon (nudge first, then stop, cut at the last sound; the horizon is doubled and the nudge repeated once while the family reads held true); a maximum length (nudge at 3 h 45 min, stop at 4 h unless extended); sleep; low disk space (REC20). What the machine does not contain: a starting phase that can end before the recorder arms, a first reading treated as an edge, a terminal ended phase, pair or lone dwells, a reprompt cooldown, a skip latch, bundle-keyed candidates, a confidence score.

CONSTANTS, all per-family values in the repo root/daemon/Sources/MeetingPipe/Resources/meeting_apps.toml and each measured in shadow before it acts. D: 3 s for Teams and Zoom, 5 s for a browser showing an identified meeting page, 20 s for a browser with no identified page, 30 s for an unlisted app. G: 60 s at the flip, which equals the current build's pair dwell so the flip changes the authority and not the timing; then set once from the uncensored ledger to the larger of 30 s and 1.25 times the longest same-call gap that returned, capped at 60 s. The two known same-call gaps are 23.1 s between rows (2026-11-12, re-derived) and at most 23.5 s (2026-10-22, inherited), sampled on a poll that can add up to 5 s, so 30 s is inside measurement error today. R: 120 s. release_means_end per family: yes (Teams; inherited basis: 38,212 s of Teams recording under a muted verdict with the flag true against 1.3 s false outside end-of-call tails, with the caveat that the mute verdict itself was wrong for part of that time), ambiguous (Zoom until its probe passes, Slack, classic Teams, unlisted apps, any browser that fails its mute bar), never (Webex).

FAR-SIDE CHECK (the two critics disagreed; this is the resolution). While the system channel carried speech-level audio in the last 30 s, a releasing recording does not finalize; the HUD says the app released the microphone but call audio is still playing and offers Still in call and Finish now. It only delays: it never moves the cut, which stays at the release unless the owner presses Still in call, so room talk or other audio after a call cannot reach the transcript or the summarizer. It is capped at 600 s after the release. It ships only after the shadow journal shows that at real ends the system channel goes quiet inside G in at least 90 percent of releases; the system channel is display-wide and the pack has no audio, so this cannot be replayed today. At the cap a call that went on with the microphone released is still lost from the transcript (the audio is kept under the default posture); that is listed as a remaining failure.

CUT. The mark is computed after the stop, from the session journal, on every stop route, so no route can skip it: the release sample plus 2 s (exact), the owner's Finish now (exact), app exit (exact), or the last sound plus 5 s for a silence-based stop (estimated). A weak-signal time is never a mark. Under the default posture the file keeps the tail, the transcript, summary and sinks stop at the mark (the existing held_end_trim_sec and HeldEndTrim), and Restore is one click. Under regulated or NDA the same mark truncates on disk as today; that posture is off on this laptop, and whether an estimated mark may truncate is an owner question.

REJOIN AND HAND START. A microphone return inside G is the same recording with no mark. A return of the same family within R of a recorded close starts a new recording without an offer, linked by resumed_from and never merged automatically (ADR 0017's 2026-10-24 choice), but with the workflow resolved afresh instead of pinned from the previous recording (audit F046). The microphone cannot tell a dropped call that came back from the next meeting, so this is put to the owner; the alternative is an offer with Continue preselected. After R a new span is a new session with its own offer. A gap of 10 s or more that came back is written as a marker so a fused pair can be split later. A hand start always works: it binds to the open session if there is exactly one (the frontmost family's if several), otherwise it records unbound and adopts the first session that opens. Only the session bound to a recording can end it. The session's decision is written to a small journal so a relaunch inside 5 minutes reuses it instead of asking again.

SECOND OPENER (a condition of the pick, from both critics). The scanner keeps running while idle. A scan that stays confident for 60 s while the family's microphone reads false or unknown raises a quiet offer, never an automatic recording, keyed one per confident interval so a title flap cannot repeat it, and logs attribution_suspect. A recording started from it uses the ambiguous end rule keyed on scan confidence. This covers audio-off joins and a vendor moving capture to a process the matcher misses, which would otherwise read false forever with a green health line.

WHEN UNSURE. A closed list; none of these stops the recorder by itself. (1) The microphone state cannot be read for more than 10 s: 'Recording. Can't read Teams' microphone state'; the grace clock is paused; the recording continues until it reads again, the owner stops, or a ceiling. (2) An ambiguous-release family released the microphone: 'Zoom released the microphone. Still in the call?' with Keep recording as the default and Finish; unanswered, it finalizes after 120 s of silence on both channels, cut at the last sound, labelled estimated. (3) Browsers only: the microphone is still held but the identified meeting page has been gone from every window for 60 s: the HUD says so and offers Finish and Keep; no automatic end except the ceilings. (4) A holder that cannot be named as a meeting (a browser with no identified page, an unlisted app): a quiet offer, never recorded automatically, with Not a meeting and Never for this app. (5) The far-side check above. A healthy Leave control, a present window or a matching title while the microphone reads false extends nothing: those readings were wrong after a real Zoom leave on 2026-11-12 (the hold opened at 15:19:45Z was reverted at 15:19:53Z, 4 s after the microphone release at 15:19:49Z).

MIC AND MUTE. 'Is an app on the microphone, and which one' is answered by the monitor, at idle, while offering and while recording, so there is no separate mic-in-use tier and no device bound once at launch. The holder's device list is compared with the recorder's input and a mismatch is shown on the HUD during the call. Mute is metadata (ADR 0016) and can no longer start, shape or stop a recording. Four repairs: a Teams log line is applied only if its own timestamp is within 5 s of now, older lines are history (inherited: 16 of 17 mute contradictions longer than 5 s began at a log line, median line age 41.9 s); the newest reading wins and the button is sampled as a level so a refused reading is seen again a second later; an unreadable control yields unknown and is shown as unknown instead of being cleared to unmuted (whether a confirmed mute should hold under regulated is an owner decision on ADR 0016); the first gate verdict of every recording is always published. Browser mute and most of Zoom's stay unknown and say so.

HEALTH. Every sampler writes a heartbeat row each minute and raises sampler_silent after 10 s without a tick; a CI fence fails when a sampler is constructed and never ticked (the END10 class); a down reading counts only after an up in the same session; an input that has never produced a sample shows as never read in doctor and the menu. One recording_end_decision row per recording holds the route, each input's last reading and its age, and the cut.

**The strongest argument against this recommendation.** The strongest case against this recommendation is that most of it should not be built. The measured losses on this laptop were dominated by two things that are not detection-design problems: the unanswered prompt that turns into Skip (inherited: 69 percent of lost or late call seconds, with the microphone reading true at 29 of 30 Teams timeouts) and one vendor title change handled badly by one old build (26 percent, already neutralised since END14). A settings change removes the first today. In the newest build, 0 of 6 recordings were ended early by a wrong proxy, the 4 trims were all correct, and the two defects actually seen (the resume race and the first-stamp cut) are small patches on the existing structure. On that reading the rational plan is stages 0 to 7 and then stop: the session reducer buys a nicer one-minute countdown and a browser path that may not exist, at the price of three to four weeks, a rewrite of the most incident-prone 1,980 lines in the app, and a new single point of trust. That single point is weaker than it looks. Every one of the 495 microphone rows was logged while the old detector was already engaged, so the flag's behaviour outside detected calls is unknown; none came from the per-object listener; the grace rests on two gaps of about 23 s measured through a poll that adds up to 5 s; Zoom is 3 calls; browsers are 0 rows. Nine END tasks in this codebase each looked right on paper and each moved the failure sideways, and this design overturns a standing rule (process audio never ends a recording alone) that was kept deliberately. A Teams or macOS update that moves capture to another process would take out start and end together. And the first-named pain may be untouched: if bar B1 fails, or the employer forbids new grants, Google Meet in a browser stays a title match on the selected tab, however elegant the native design is. I still recommend the staged path because the plan makes this dissent testable instead of rhetorical. Nothing before the gate changes how a Teams recording ends except three small patches and one Teams-only stop; the reducer runs in shadow; and the gate before stages 10 and 11 keeps Teams on the patched engine by default if two weeks show no early stops, no overruns and no unrecorded calls. The reducer is built anyway for one reason the dissent does not answer: it is the only route to a browser call that is caught by something better than a tab title, and the browser's current end path is a single weak signal with a 12 minute wait. What I would not accept from the dissent is stopping before stage 5: without the always-on ledger nobody, including the next audit, can count a missed call.
