# Recording start and stop reliability: research and designs

Companion to the independent audit of 2026-11-12 (`2026-11-12-independent-audit.md`, in this folder), whose executive summary carries the conclusions drawn from this file. Appendix to `2026-11-13-recording-reliability-audit.md`.

## How other recorders detect meetings

### Open-source recorders (source read)

**Does anyone reach 100 percent?** No, not among the open-source projects I read, and several say so themselves. OpenOats' maintainer closed the Google Meet in a browser request by calling it a platform limitation and pointing to the PWA workaround (issue 187), and the app produced 11 false recordings in 45 minutes from dictation before a veto was added (issue 726). pasrom/meeting-transcriber, which has the most deliberate detector of the set, has an open report of Google Meet in Chrome not being picked up (issue 814), an open Safari gap (563), and three closed misses caused by vendors renaming assertions (510, 562, 652). screenpipe has had the detector stuck flapping between Active and Ending (4525), a session that kept transcribing after the call because the close failed (7705), sessions force-ended at two hours (7879), and misattribution between browser tabs (6583). OpenWhispr's own documentation calls calendar the most reliable trigger and tells users to start by hotkey if prompts never appear. Anarlog shipped an auto-stop reliability fix in 1.0.27 and, from the code I read, ends a session through a notification that stops on timeout instead of ending it directly. Recap has no end handling at all. What these projects have in common is not a perfect sensor but containment: a short confirmation in front of the start, a grace period and a default-stop prompt on the end, a backstop behind both, and a manual control that always works. I did not examine closed-source products in this pass, so this answer says nothing about them. My honest read is that 100 percent is not available on macOS for a browser-hosted call without a browser extension, because no public API reports which tab is in a call; what is available is a detector whose misses are rare, visible and cheap to correct.

- Nobody I read relies on one signal. The common shape is: a cheap CoreAudio trigger, then an identity step (which app, which site), then a human confirmation or a default-stop notification, then a blunt backstop (OpenOats 900 s silence, pasrom 14400 s ceiling, screenpipe 2 hour cap, OpenWhispr 60 s joint silence).
- Per-process microphone input from the CoreAudio process object list (kAudioHardwarePropertyProcessObjectList plus kAudioProcessPropertyIsRunningInput) is the consensus trigger: Anarlog, pasrom, screenpipe (its default backend on macOS) and OpenWhispr all use it, and OpenOats added it as a veto. Not one project uses AVCaptureDevice.isInUseByAnotherApplication, which is the input this project's MicBusySpanTracker depends on and which its own docs say has produced zero spans (DET10).
- Device-level kAudioDevicePropertyDeviceIsRunningSomewhere is used only as a wake-up edge, and the careful implementations listen on every input device or re-register on kAudioHardwarePropertyDefaultInputDevice changes and keep a 1 to 5 s poll behind the listener (Anarlog POLL_INTERVAL 1 s with a polling fallback, OpenWhispr 5 s heartbeat). It also reflects the recorder's own capture, so during a recording only the per-process list minus self carries information.
- For Google Meet in a plain browser tab I found four strategies. Do not identify the tab at all and treat any sustained browser mic use as a meeting (Anarlog's start path, OpenWhispr). Refuse and tell the user to install the PWA (OpenOats). Read the page URL through Accessibility or AppleScript once the browser holds the mic (screenpipe: AXDocument on the window, AppleScript active-tab probe; Anarlog reads AXURL on the web area, though I could not establish that it gates start). Watch the Chromium power assertion named 'WebRTC has active PeerConnections' through IOPMCopyAssertionsByProcess (pasrom). Title-only matching (Recap, and this project today) is the weakest of the set.
- The local Google Meet start matcher requires 'meet' plus a 3-4-3 room code in the window title. Three other projects encode Meet titles that carry the event name and no code (Anarlog test fixture 'Team sync - Google Meet - Google Chrome', pasrom issue 503 'Standup - Google Meet', Recap's 'google meet' pattern). If the owner's scheduled meetings are titled that way, the tab is classified noMeetingSignal and skipped silently, with no browser_tab_rejected row. This is a hypothesis from other projects' fixtures, not a capture from this machine, and it is cheap to check by reading the tab title during a calendar-scheduled call.
- The local plain-browser end path has a single evidence class. The repo's own corpus file google_meet_browser_clean_leave.json says the active tab title is the only end signal, that it flips on any tab switch, and that the end confirms on the 12 minute lone dwell (PromotionEngine.defaultLoneDwell = 720) unless the idle backstop fires first, after which the recording is trimmed to the hold start. Every other project has a second, tab-independent signal for the browser case and ends in 5 to 20 s.
- End grace periods elsewhere are short: 0 s (OpenOats mic release), 5 s after mic release plus quiet system audio (OpenWhispr, only after a 30 s hold), 15 s (pasrom), 20 s (screenpipe). The price is visible in their trackers: screenpipe has Active to Ending flapping bugs and AirPods-induced flaps. This project's 60 s pair dwell is conservative by comparison; the gap is the missing second signal for browsers, not the dwell length.
- The best-engineered detail I saw for back-to-back meetings is call identity: pasrom tracks AssertionId sets and splits the recording when every known ID is gone and only new ones remain (stopNewCall), and screenpipe treats a changed conference-room URL as an immediate boundary. This project's docs name the lack of a meeting-instance ID as a known blind spot of the skip latch.
- Safari needs special handling everywhere: its power assertion is 'WebKit Media Playback' for calls and for video alike (pasrom issue 563), and its mic is held by a com.apple.WebKit.GPU helper that is neither a child of Safari nor a bundle-ID prefix match. Two projects map it with responsibility_get_pid_responsible_for_pid, which is exported but not public API. The local ProcessAudioSignal.match uses a bundle prefix and a parent-PID walk, so it would not attribute Safari's helper.
- Signals that depend on vendor strings fail silently when the vendor changes them: Zoom renamed its assertion to a sample-code placeholder (pasrom 562), Webex uses 'On a call' (652), screenpipe had to drop over-broad strings such as 'google meet' as a URL pattern and 'end' as a button name.
- The confirmation step is itself a failure point. pasrom's open Meet bug (814) and issue 543 were a consent notification that was posted and never seen. Anarlog stops on notification timeout, so a missed notification ends the recording; OpenOats auto-records only after an informational notification is confirmed posted.
- Sleep and wake are handled explicitly by the better ones: OpenOats ends on NSWorkspace.willSleepNotification, OpenWhispr treats a tick gap above 5 s as sleep and resets its quiet timers so a wake cannot instantly satisfy a silence window.

**Applicable here.** Scope note: I read this repo only to anchor the comparison and ran nothing. Every local claim below comes from the source or docs, and the items marked probe are unmeasured. What this project already has that the others lack: a per-process input signal with a resolver (ProcessAudioSignal, ProcessAudioScanProbe), an AX Leave anchor, ScreenCaptureKit window presence, a held end with trim (END17), a replayable detection corpus and a probe-first process. The deltas are narrow and almost all on the plain-browser path. Delta 1, browser Meet end has one evidence class. BrowserMeetingLifecycleAdapter fuses only ShareableContentSignal (window title) and WorkspaceSignal (browser quit) for plain browsers, so a title-left end can only confirm on the 720 s lone dwell or the idle backstop. Candidates for a second class, ranked by whether they survive the most common posture (muted, looking at another tab): (a) Chromium WebRTC power assertion. API: IOPMCopyAssertionsByProcess (IOKit, no TCC grant), match assertions whose name contains 'WebRTC has active PeerConnections' on the browser's process; pasrom issue 814 shows it present for Chrome 156 on macOS 27.0.1 as a NoIdleSleepAssertion. It is independent of tab focus. Expected to be independent of mute because a muted call keeps its PeerConnection (my inference). Chromium family only. It names the browser, not the site, so it should corroborate a Meet identity obtained elsewhere, never start a recording alone. Probe before adopting: is it held in the pre-join lobby, does it drop on Leave or only when the post-call page is closed, and how long after. (b) All-tab URL enumeration over AppleScript or JXA (URL of every tab of every window, supported by Chrome-family browsers and Safari). Survives tab switches and mute. Needs the Automation permission per browser and does not work for Firefox (my inference from the scripting model; screenpipe uses AppleScript only for the active tab and documents about 100 ms per call with a 3 s timeout). (c) Browser process mic release, by extending the existing ProcessAudioSignal to browser bundle IDs. Independent of tab focus, but the local header already warns that an input drop can be mute-induced, and whether Chrome's running-input flag drops when Meet is muted is unmeasured. Paired with a tab-switch title flip it could complete the 60 s pair dwell in the middle of a call. Treat as a probe, and if adopted keep it as a vetoer (it already is: vetoesLoneEnd) before trusting it as an end class. (d) AXURL or AXDocument no longer on meet.google.com. Active tab per window only (Anarlog's background-tab test pins this), so it does not fix tab switches; it fixes identity. Delta 2, browser Meet start identity. Today admission needs a title with the room code, and the URL-fragment disjunct reads the window title, which never contains the URL (the DET6 spike says this itself). Two shipping projects read the URL through Accessibility, which this app already holds: screenpipe reads AXDocument on the browser's AXWindow, Anarlog reads AXURL on the AXWebArea and falls back to an http-prefixed AXValue, noting that AXURL arrives as a CFURL on some browsers and a CFString on others. That is evidence that DET6's leg 1 is worth running now, and it tells the probe exactly which attributes and element levels to read first. It is not a verdict: the spike's tree is pre-registered and should be decided by the owner's captures. Matching rule worth copying from Anarlog's browser_platform_from_url: https only, exact host, never a substring, so a search results page about Meet cannot match. Separately, check the hypothesis in the takeaways about named meeting titles by reading the tab title during a scheduled call; if confirmed, the cheapest fix is the URL, not a looser title matcher (which would reopen END5). Delta 3, start under a muted join. ARCHITECTURE.md says joining muted must still be detected, so browser mic input cannot be the sole trigger the way it is in Anarlog and OpenWhispr. The power assertion and the URL do not depend on the mic. A workable fusion in this codebase's terms: the discovery scan adds a browser candidate when any of {WebRTC assertion on a browser process, browser process running input} is true, and admits it only when the URL (or today's strong title) says meet.google.com with a room path. That is screenpipe's rule (trigger, then resolve) with a second trigger that works muted. Delta 4, mic-in-use. Replace the AVCaptureDevice.isInUseByAnotherApplication observation in MeetingDiscoveryWatcher (resolved once at start, zero spans per DET10) with what every other project uses: an AudioObjectAddPropertyListenerBlock on kAudioDevicePropertyDeviceIsRunningSomewhere for each input device from kAudioHardwarePropertyDevices, re-enumerated on device-list and kAudioHardwarePropertyDefaultInputDevice changes, with a slow poll behind it, and attribution from the process object list this project already enumerates. Treat the device flag as an idle wake-up only, because it includes this app's own capture once recording starts. Borrow two filters: skip com.apple.CoreSpeech (OpenWhispr) and a dictation bundle list (OpenOats), so MicInUseTier does not prompt for dictation. Delta 5, Safari attribution. If the owner uses Safari, ProcessAudioSignal.match needs a third rule after bundle prefix and parent-PID walk: responsibility_get_pid_responsible_for_pid, since com.apple.WebKit.GPU is neither. It is exported but not public API, so it is a deliberate dependency decision, and Anarlog's alternative (walk the executable path up to the outermost .app) does not help for a system XPC service. Delta 6, smaller borrowings. Call identity for back-to-back meetings: the Meet room code from the URL is a free meeting-instance ID for the skip latch's documented blind spot, and pasrom's AssertionId set does the same for Teams. A tick-gap sleep guard like OpenWhispr's TICK_GAP_MS for the hold clocks. OpenWhispr's vanish-race rule (a process object that disappears mid-read is skipped and its PID kept until the next reconcile) fits ProcessAudioSignal's resolver. One probe would settle most of this in the repo's own spike shape: during a real Meet call in the owner's browser, log once a second (1) IOPMCopyAssertionsByProcess entries for the browser, (2) running-input for the browser and its helpers, (3) AXDocument and AXURL per browser window, (4) window titles, across lobby, join muted, unmute, switch tab, mute, leave to the post-call screen, close the tab. Pre-register which signal must be true through the whole call and false within N seconds of leaving. Not recommended from this evidence: shortening the 60 s dwell, widening strongTitleMatchers, or adopting any single signal as a solo end.

### Commercial products

**Does anyone reach 100 percent?** No. No botless desktop recorder claims it, and the evidence runs the other way. Recall.ai, which sells detection to other recorders, published fixes during 2026 for false positive, phantom, spurious and delayed detections, pre-join and waiting rooms read as meetings, a Teams build that broke detection, false meeting-closed events (Meet twice, Zoom on an audio disconnect, Slack on a mic change, Chromium on a tab switch), missing meeting-closed events for Teams, and browser calls that stayed open after they ended; its FAQ also documents structural misses (a Meet in a background tab, a Zoom window on another Space). Granola's help center says back-to-back calls can merge, that talk after hang-up keeps transcription running, and that pressing stop is the most reliable end for ad hoc meetings. MacWhisper labels its detection beta and recommends manual recording for critical meetings. Wispr Flow documents both early stops and recordings left running. The only recorders with ground truth are the meeting clients themselves, and even Teams has a forgot-to-leave rule that restarts the recording every four hours. What the competitors achieve is a feeling of reliability, built from five choices: a prompt instead of a silent auto-start, so a wrong detection costs a dismissed notification; a cheap UI-independent trigger (which process holds the mic); an end rule that requires several signals plus a grace period and otherwise asks; a visible always-on indicator with a one-click stop that is treated as normal use; and a bound on the damage (silence timeout, length cap, resume or merge). Limits of this research: Krisp's help center sits behind a sign-in, the Otter and OpenAI help pages blocked direct fetches and were read through search excerpts, Talat publishes no behaviour docs, and no first-hand user report of a multi-hour runaway recording was located (only vendor docs describing the mechanism).

- Nobody outside the meeting client reaches 100 percent, and the vendor with a public detection changelog shows it: Recall.ai's release notes list fixes for missed, phantom, prematurely closed and never-closed meetings in almost every release from December 2025 to November 2026, including two rounds of guardrails against false Google Meet closes on macOS. Only Zoom, Teams and Meet themselves have ground truth, because they are the call.
- The start signal most botless products lean on is the same one: a known app's process is holding the microphone. Granola, Notion, Otter, Circleback, Jamie and Hedy all document it. It needs no Accessibility grant and does not depend on a client's UI, titles or locale. It fails toward false prompts (dictation, voice chat, a video message), which is why it is paired with app allow and deny lists and made a prompt.
- A prompt, not a silent auto-start, is the default wherever a default is documented: Granola, Notion, MacWhisper and Hedy never auto-start; Otter defaults to Remind; Jamie and Wispr Flow ship auto-start switched off; Circleback and Fireflies offer it as an optional setting. Where auto-start exists it demands a second signal (a calendar event inside a time window for Jamie, a recognized conference tab for Wispr Flow, a high-confidence match for BuildBetter) and shows a cancelable countdown.
- The end is decided by a conjunction plus a grace period, and when unsure the products ask or keep recording. Jamie needs mic released and 30 seconds of silence and the last quarter of the calendar event, otherwise a 10 minute failsafe, and by default only notifies. Otter and Circleback show Keep recording. Hedy asks and never ends by itself. Wispr Flow asks when a mute or device switch looks like a leave, and ignoring the question keeps recording. Granola's backstop is 15 minutes of silence.
- Manual stop is the documented norm, not a failure. Granola calls the stop button the most reliable way to end an ad hoc transcription, MacWhisper tells users to record critical meetings by hand, and tl;dv, Fireflies, ChatGPT and Notion all describe a manual stop first. An always-visible controller (Otter) and a hard length cap (ChatGPT 4 hours, Wispr Flow 2 hours by default) bound what a missed end can cost.
- Calendar anchoring is the biggest reliability lever competitors have: it tells them a meeting is expected, gates auto-start, names the note, and gives the end a horizon (Granola, Jamie, Wispr Flow, Fathom, ChatGPT). This project measured that lever as unavailable for its owner (ADR 0011 and CAL1: EventKit covered 0 of 55 recorded meetings because the work calendar is not connected to macOS), so it has to win on the other signals.
- Google Meet in a browser is everyone's weakest case, and the mic cannot fix it alone because a browser on the mic names the browser, never the tab. Recall.ai only detects a Meet whose tab is visibly on screen or in picture-in-picture, supports a named browser list, and in November 2026 was still fixing Meet calls that stayed open while another tab used the mic. Wispr Flow ends reliably only on a Chromium tab close and leaves the recording running when it cannot confirm one. The products with the best Meet fidelity also ship a Chrome extension (Notion, Granola, Krisp), documented for speaker labels and consent.
- Measured against these, this project's end policy (ADR 0017: hold, keep recording, trim later) is already more conservative than anything a competitor documents. What it lacks is the cheap signal they all use, a per-process mic hold for browsers and unlisted apps at both ends, and a one-click question at the moment an end is suspected. The repo excluded browsers from that read on purpose and parked the question behind DET6's unrun measurement; today a plain browser Meet has one weak end class, so a normal leave waits out the 12 minute lone dwell or the 900 second idle backstop.

**Applicable here.** Deltas only; the repo already has the Record or Skip prompt, the HUD, the 480 s nudge and 900 s auto-stop, and hold-then-trim. Paths are under the repo root.

1. Revisit the deliberate exclusion of browsers from the process-audio read, and re-band DET6. The per-process Core Audio read is live for Teams and Zoom (`ProcessAudioScanProbe` and `ProcessAudioSignal` over `kAudioHardwarePropertyProcessObjectList`, `kAudioProcessPropertyBundleID`, `kAudioProcessPropertyPID`, `kAudioProcessPropertyIsRunningInput`). `MeetingSourceScanner.readsProcessAudio` is native-only on purpose: its comment and DET12 record that a browser on the mic names the browser and never the tab, and DET6 owns whether a browser tab may be admitted on that evidence, gated on a measurement nobody has run. DET6 is P2 because the corpus held 0 browser meetings, and its own header says to re-band to P1 the moment a browser meeting is wanted, which is now. The competitor evidence is that everyone accepts the per-browser ambiguity as a cost and contains it rather than avoiding the signal: Granola, Notion, Otter, Circleback and Jamie trigger on it, Wispr Flow ends a Mac browser call on it, and Recall.ai's 2.0.44 fix (Meet calls staying open while another tab or app uses the mic) is exactly the failure to design for. So use it only as a corroborator: at start it upgrades a weak Meet or brand title to an admission and never admits alone; at end it is one weak class, never definitive. DET6 already notes that DET8's bundle-prefix matching would give a browser helper the same read. Probe-first, unmeasured: which process object holds the input per browser (Inference: a Chromium helper under the browser's bundle prefix; Safari capture in a WebKit process whose bundle ID is not Safari-prefixed, which would need matching by responsible or parent PID), and whether Meet's in-call mute releases the input (Wispr Flow documents that a mute can look like a leave).

2. Unblock the mic-in-use catch-all. DET10 records that `AVCaptureDevice.isInUseByAnotherApplication` has produced zero spans, so `MicInUseTier` never fires and a call the title matcher misses never prompts; the probe (`daemon/scripts/det10-mic-busy-probe.swift`) is owner-owed and its pre-registered NO-GO branch already names `kAudioHardwarePropertyProcessObjectList` as the replacement, with `kAudioDevicePropertyDeviceIsRunningSomewhere` as the corroborator. Whatever the verdict, move the holder attribution off the frontmost app and onto the process object list: that answers the reasons the mic-busy substitution was rejected on 2026-07-20 (system-wide, cannot name the holder, Webex ultrasound), and it is what lets Granola and Hedy put the app's name in the prompt. Keep it a prompt, and add a deny list for dictation and voice tools, which is how Jamie (unrecognized mic users never trigger), Krisp (an app exclusion list) and Fireflies (Ignored apps) contain the false prompts Recall.ai warns about.

3. Widen what the Meet matcher can see. `collectAXWindowTitles` reads each browser window's `kAXTitleAttribute`, which is the selected tab's page title, and `MeetingTitlePatterns.googleMeet` requires the Meet brand plus a 3-4-3 room code. So a Meet that is not the selected tab of its window is invisible, and (Inference, to verify with a title log) a scheduled Meet whose tab is titled with the event name instead of the code fails the matcher; Recall.ai's fixes for Meet tab titles containing a colon and for Meet title detection suggest free-text titles are normal. Options, in cost order: run DET6's probe, which already tests the address bar `AXValue` and the web area's `AXURL` and `AXDocument`; enumerate the tab strip through AX so background tabs count (probe-first, not verified in this repo); or read tab URLs over Apple Events under the Automation grant, the mechanism BuildBetter documents and which Recall.ai's `browser-automation` permission suggests it also uses. Then admit on the Meet brand in any tab plus the browser holding the mic, not on the room code alone. Recall.ai documents the same visible-tab limit, so AX alone will not close this; delta 1 is what does.

4. Give a browser leave a real pair. The browser adapter wires only `ShareableContentSignal` and `WorkspaceSignal` for plain browsers, so a leave or a tab close is one weak class and runs to the 12 minute lone dwell. With delta 1, Meet title gone from every readable tab plus browser released the mic is a cross-class pair that confirms on the existing 60 s `confirm_dwell`, with the trim point at hold start. Keep both in the weak tier and keep the live-mic veto: Recall.ai needed guardrails against false Meet closes on macOS twice in 2026 and a fix for tab switches ending meetings, and Wispr Flow's rule when a tab close cannot be confirmed is to keep recording.

5. Ask at hold start. Jamie (Notification only by default), Otter and Circleback (Keep recording), Hedy and Wispr Flow all turn a suspected end into a one-click question. The HUD already reads Confirming end; add actions there or on a notification: Stop now, which routes to the definitive user Stop with the trim at hold start, and Keep recording, which reverts the hold and quiets that signal class for a while. Timeout behaviour stays as ADR 0017 has it (keep holding), which matches Wispr Flow's rule that ignoring the question keeps recording.

6. Consider a short two-channel silence window as an evidence class inside a hold (Inference from Jamie's and Otter's rules; probe-first). `IdleStopBackstop` already uses both channels at the long horizon: live system audio resets the streak and a down system channel holds it. Jamie's rule is the short version, 30 s without speech as a required leg of the stop. Far-side audio going silent within seconds of a leave is UI-independent and needs no new permission, so mic idle plus system channel silent for the 60 s dwell could pair with a weak end where no second class exists. Measure on the existing corpus how often both channels are silent for 60 s or more mid-meeting first.

7. Calendar anchoring stays out, on the existing measurement (ADR 0011, CAL1). If the owner ever connects the work calendar to macOS, Jamie's rule (final quarter of the event for the stop, 5 minutes before to 10 after for the start) is the one to copy.

8. Ground truth for browser Meet needs code in the page. A small browser extension with native messaging that reports join, leave and mute per tab is the only route to first-party-grade truth; Notion, Granola and Krisp already ship Meet extensions for other purposes. It is a new dependency and install step, so it is an owner decision, and worth taking only if deltas 1, 3 and 4 measure badly.

9. Bound a missed end. Live system audio resets the idle streak, so after a missed end anything that keeps playing (a video, music, the next call) keeps the recording alive with no ceiling. ChatGPT caps a session at 4 hours and Wispr Flow at 2 hours by default with a warning. A length check-in (a nudge, not a stop) would cover that case.

### macOS signals available

**Does anyone reach 100 percent?** No, and the vendors say so themselves. Granola's documentation states that it never starts without a click, that ad hoc auto-stop depends on the call app releasing the microphone with a 15 minute no-audio timeout behind it, that back-to-back meetings can be merged because the first end is not detected, that non-admin users below macOS 14.2 may not get the call-ended signal, and that pressing stop is the most reliable way to end. Hyprnote's open source detector polls the per-process mic list once a second and confirms a browser call by finding an English leave call button in the selected tab's accessibility tree, which fails on a localized Meet UI (the owner's Meet buttons are in Ukrainian per the repo's own probe) and cannot see a non-selected tab. The platform itself offers no public API that says a call is live or which tab holds the microphone, and Google's event API gives the ended event only to the meeting space owner. What the best available combination still cannot see: a Meet session joined without a microphone (companion or phone-audio joins, inferred); Firefox tab URLs; a second tab in the same browser that keeps the mic after the call tab releases it; and anything on a day when Meet changes its page and an extension's selector breaks. A realistic target is different from 100 percent detection: never chop a live meeting (the repo's hold-long-trim-later policy already aims at that), never miss a browser call that uses the mic (process-object trigger plus tab identity), state the end as an event rather than infer it where an extension is installed, and keep the manual hotkey as the documented fallback, which is exactly what the leading competitor does.

- The repo sees a browser meeting only through window titles, and a browser window's title is the selected tab's title. A Meet tab that is not the selected tab of its window cannot be detected by any read the app does today, and switching away from the Meet tab reads as the meeting window disappearing. That one fact explains both rare Meet detection and unreliable browser ends.
- The one permission-free signal that can notice a call in a non-selected tab, per-process IsRunningInput on the browser's audio process, is switched off for browsers in MeetingSourceScanner.readsProcessAudio. It cannot name the tab, but it is the right trigger for a tab identification pass.
- Verified from source: Chromium runs audio in a separate helper on macOS and WebKit captures the mic in its GPU process. So the HAL object to watch is a browser helper, not the browser's main process, and Safari needs an explicit rule because a WebKit service will not carry a Safari bundle prefix (that last part is inferred and needs a probe).
- The only source-verified way to read the URL of a non-selected tab without an extension is the browser's scripting dictionary over Apple Events (Chrome's sdef exposes URL and title for every tab of every window). It costs one new Automation consent per browser. Firefox has no such dictionary.
- Chromium builds a per-tab accessible label with a localized recording suffix for tabs that capture the mic or camera (read in Chromium 128 source, still called per tab on main). If macOS Accessibility exposes unselected tab elements, that gives tab-level mic attribution on the Accessibility grant the app already holds. This is unmeasured and is the cheapest probe to run next.
- A content-script extension on the Meet page with native messaging is the only channel that reports join and leave as events and exposes Meet's mute state. Granola ships one for Chromium browsers. It needs no macOS grant but is a new artifact per browser.
- Device-level reads are fine as an idle wake-up and useless during a recording: the daemon's own capture pins the default input and output devices as running (already measured in this repo).
- AVCaptureDevice.isInUseByAnotherApplication has fired zero times on this Mac and the reason is unexplained. Replace the mic-busy tracker with the per-process object list, which also names the holder.
- Dead ends confirmed: the system privacy indicator has no public API and log scraping needs a private framework plus admin rights; Meet's event API withholds the ended event from anyone who does not own the meeting space; Teams' local third-party control capability was retired on June 30, 2026; Focus status says nothing about calls.
- Nothing fetched establishes three Meet behaviours that the design depends on: the tab title of a calendar-named meeting, the title and URL after leaving, and whether muting releases the capture stream. Each must be measured before a rule relies on it.
- No documented change to the process-object, tap, Accessibility or ScreenCaptureKit behaviour was found for macOS 26 or 27. Apple's current Core Audio taps sample page is dated for macOS 26 and still describes the same API and the same usage-description key.

## The three designs considered

### Mic Session: one sampled per-process microphone level, with hysteresis, that every start, offer and end binds to (mic-truth-first)

Stop inferring a call from another vendor's interface and read the one call fact macOS states per process: is this meeting app, or this browser's audio process, capturing microphone input right now. Sample that level once a second from the CoreAudio process-object list (no permission needed). A session opens when the level has read true for 3 s and closes when it has read false for 30 s. The offer, a decline, the recording and its end all belong to that session and to nothing else. Accessibility, window titles, tab titles and the Teams log only name the meeting, route it to a workflow and break ties; none of them can open a session, and none can end a recording. Why this and not one more rule: in the work-laptop pack (re-derived this run: 114,553 events, 74 recordings, 69 of them Teams or Zoom) the mic level was the only end signal that was ever right. Of 102 provisional ends, 59 were led by the mic release and 58 of those were real ends or real call drops (the other was a 23.1 s gap that came back; that none of the 58 was a false end rests on this run's earlier field classification of the 7 early stops, which is inherited). 31 were led by a missing Leave control: 29 reverted by themselves, 1 chopped a live call, 1 coincided with a hand stop. 11 were led by a vanished window: 4 chopped live calls, 7 reverted. In both mid-call mic gaps on record the Leave control read invalid in the same seconds, so the second evidence class that ADR 0017 demands never told the two cases apart; only duration did. Across 38,845 s of Teams and Zoom recording time that the gate judged app-muted, the flag read false for 178 s, 177 of them after the call's final release and 1 inside the 23.1 s gap, so in-app mute did not release it. On the start side all 69 native recordings had the flag true at the start (64 before the recording began, 5 within 0.4 to 10.0 s after it), and an estimated 86 Teams mic sessions drew 407 prompts. Two things the stance does not buy, stated up front. First, at 29 of the 30 Teams prompts that timed out into Skip the last mic reading was true, so detection was already right there and the loss was the consent contract; this design therefore makes the offer live as long as the session and never turn into Skip, and leaves auto-record as the owner's decision. Second, there is no microphone evidence for any browser in the pack (0 rows), so every browser rule below is gated on a probe with bars written first. On 100 percent: nobody has it. The research in this run found no botless recorder that claims it; the vendor with a public changelog shipped fixes for missed, phantom, early-closed and never-closed meetings through 2026, and the products the owner is thinking of lean on this same signal, a known app's process holding the microphone, plus a prompt, with a manual stop documented as the reliable end. What can be had is a detector whose errors are few, visible and cheap: a late end costs 30 s of trimmed tail, a wrong end costs a linked second part, and an unsure state is shown as unsure.

**State machine.** ONE SERVICE. MicHolderMonitor (new, in MeetingPipeCore, behind the existing CoreAudioHALBus backend so tests inject a fake) runs from Coordinator.start() as a SessionWatchers member. Every second it enumerates the process-object list, keeps the objects whose bundle id belongs to a registered family, reads IsRunningInput on each and publishes one Sample per family: {family, held: true | false | unknown, pids, at}. held is true if any readable object reads true; false if at least one object was readable and all read false, or the family has no audio object at all; unknown only if the list or every matched read failed, or for up to 5 s after a holding object vanished (re-resolve first, the DET8 churn case). The daemon's own pid is excluded. A sample older than 3 s is treated as unknown. Cost measured this run: 0.3 ms to enumerate and about 3 ms to read all 32 objects from Python, so well under half a percent of one core at 1 Hz with no backoff. The monitor logs a row on every change of held and on every return after a release (mic_gap with the exact gap), which is also the reliability ledger. ONE REDUCER. MicSession.decide(state, event, now) returns the next state and a list of effects (show offer, hide offer, start recorder, stop recorder with cause and cut, log). Pure, in Core, no clocks of its own. Events are Sample, owner actions, app exit and wake. One state per family; at most one recording, bound to exactly one session. CONSTANTS. D = 3 s open debounce for Teams and Zoom, 5 s for a browser showing a known meeting page, 20 s for a browser with no identified page, 30 s for an unlisted app. G = 30 s close grace. R = 120 s rejoin window (the existing ResumeAfterEnd value). Ceiling = the configured idle horizon (default 900 s), doubled while the bound session reads held true. STATES AND TRANSITIONS. (1) idle. held true: go to arming(since = at). (2) arming. held false: back to idle (a blip, nothing was shown). held unknown: stay, the clock does not advance. held true for D: open a Session {id, family, pids, openedAt, consent = undecided}. Then, in this order: if another family's recording is running, track the session silently and offer it when that recording ends; if a hand-started recording with no session is running and this is the only open session, bind the recording to it and go to recording; if a recording of this family closed less than R ago with consent accepted, start a new recording without an offer as its own library row, logged resumed_from and never merged automatically (rejoin; this inherits consent across what may be the next meeting, so step 6 puts it to the owner, the alternative being an offer with Continue preselected); if the family is set to always record, start the recorder and go to recording; otherwise go to offered. (3) offered. The offer exists for as long as the session does. It has no timeout into Skip; after 30 s the corner panel collapses to a menu-bar line ('Teams call, not recording') that keeps a Record row. Record at any time: start the recorder, go to recording. Skip or Not a meeting: go to declined. The existing default_prompt_action key may still say 'record after N seconds unanswered'; it may no longer say skip. (4) declined. Nothing is asked again until the session closes; the menu-bar line stays truthful. A hand Record still works and binds to this session. (5) recording. held true: stay. held unknown for more than 10 s: raise the unsure flag (see when_unsure), recorder keeps running. Stop by the owner: stop now with cause user_stop; the session becomes declined so the still-held mic does not offer again. (6) releasing, entered from offered, declined or recording on the first held false sample, with releasedAt = that sample's time and the previous state remembered. While recording, the HUD changes at once to 'Call ended, finishing in 0:30' with Still in call and Finish now, and the cut mark is releasedAt. held true: return to the previous state, clear the mark, log mic_gap with the gap length. held unknown: pause the 30 s clock; it resumes on the next false and restarts after a wake. Still in call: return to recording with ownerHold set, which suspends automatic closing until the mic returns, the owner stops, or the ceiling. held false for G in total, or Finish now: close. (7) close. If recording: stop the recorder with cause mic_release and cut = releasedAt plus a 2 s pad. Under a lossless capture the file keeps the tail and the transcript excludes everything after the cut (the existing held_end_trim_sec and Restore); under regulated or NDA the cut is applied on disk at stop, as today. Hide any offer, forget the decline, go to idle. (8) app exit, from any state: the registered app's main process has terminated: close at once, cut at the last true sample, cause app_exited. A vanished helper process is not this; it is an unknown and then a false sample and takes the normal 30 s. (9) ceiling, while recording: both channels below level for the ceiling: stop with cause idle_ceiling, cut at the last sound. There is no stand-down branch. WHAT IS NOT IN THE MACHINE. No starting phase that can end before the recorder arms, no first reading treated as an edge, no terminal ended phase that later signals fall into, no pair or lone dwell, no hold cap, no reprompt cooldown, no skip latch, no suppressed or resume candidate keyed on a bundle id, no confidence score. A family whose release is flagged ambiguous (see end_rules) takes one extra branch in state 6: instead of closing after G it raises the unsure question and closes only on the owner, on 120 s of silence on both channels, or on the ceiling. HONEST LIMIT OF THE NUMBERS. G = 30 s sits 6.5 s above the longest mid-call gap on record, and there are only two: 23.1 s on 2026-11-12 and at most 23.5 s on 2026-10-22 (the second is an upper bound from a re-engage). Eras A and B stopped watching about a second after each release, so their gaps are censored. The first migration step measures every gap uncensored, and G is then set once to the larger of 30 s and 1.25 times the longest gap that came back.

**When unsure.** Unsure is a closed list of four conditions, each with its own words; nothing else may raise it, and none of them stops the recorder by itself. Condition 2 can finish a recording, and when it does the end is a mic release plus 120 s of silence. The idle ceiling is the single automatic end that starts from neither a mic release nor an app exit, and it is the bound on every unsure state. (1) The microphone state cannot be read for more than 10 s while recording (the list or every matched object fails, or the holder vanished and did not come back in the 5 s re-resolve). HUD and menu bar: 'Recording. Can't read Teams' microphone state'. The 30 s clock is paused. The recording continues until the mic reads again, the owner stops, or the ceiling. (2) The mic was released by a family whose release is flagged ambiguous (unmeasured apps, a browser that fails bar B2). HUD: 'Chrome released the microphone. Still in the call?' with Keep recording (default) and Finish. If nobody answers, it finishes after 120 s of silence on both channels, cut at the last sound. (3) The mic is still held but the identified meeting page has been gone from every window for 60 s (browsers only). HUD: 'The Meet tab is gone, Chrome still has the microphone' with Finish and Keep. No automatic end except the ceiling. (4) A holder that cannot be named as a meeting (a browser with no identified page, an unlisted app). A quiet offer: 'Chrome is using the microphone. Record?' with Record, Not a meeting, and Never for unidentified pages. Never auto-recorded. The ordinary grace is not an unsure state: the HUD says 'Call ended, finishing in 0:23' with Still in call and Finish now, and the menu bar reads Finishing. Deliberately not on the list: a healthy Leave control, a present window or a matching title while the mic reads false. Those readings were wrong after a real Zoom leave on 2026-11-12 and during 29 Leave flickers, so they cannot extend a session; only the mic returning or the owner can. What happens to the audio: in every unsure state the recorder keeps running and nothing is cut. A cut is only ever placed at a mic release or at the last sound before a ceiling stop, it is non-destructive under ordinary workflows (Restore brings the tail back), and it is applied on disk only under regulated or NDA, as today. The offer itself is also honest about doubt: it stays for the life of the session, collapses to a menu-bar line ('Teams call, not recording') after 30 s, and never silently becomes Skip.

**Still fails when:** The app never opens the microphone (no input device, device-level block, listen-only webinar): no session, hand start only, and no row says a call was missed. The pack cannot size it.; An app that releases the mic on mute, hold or push-to-talk for more than 30 s: a wrong end, softened to a linked second part with a gap of the release plus a few seconds. Not seen in the pack's Teams and Zoom recordings (Zoom is 3 calls with an unreadable mute control, so that is weak); unmeasured for Meet and Slack.; A mic return between 30 s and 120 s after a release is ambiguous by nature: a long device switch or a dropped call that came back, or the next meeting. The rejoin rule records it without a click and links it; the link is wrong when it was the next meeting, and the consent was inherited rather than given. Beyond 120 s it is a new offer.; Two calls in the same app less than 30 s apart with no title change: one merged recording.; The mic kept after the call (Webex, a browser helper serving another tab, a client's post-call screen): a late end bounded by the owner and the ceiling, not by detection.; Non-meeting microphone use by a registered family: a spurious offer, or a short discarded recording under always-record.; Browsers name the browser, never the tab: two meeting tabs, or a meeting plus a dictation tab, are one session. Safari attribution rests on a private call. Firefox is unseen.; The grace rests on two observed gaps. A longer, common gap shape would show up as split recordings until G is re-set from the uncensored ledger.; The offer still needs a click unless the owner chooses otherwise; a late click still loses the head of the call.; A HAL read that fails silently reads as unknown: no detection, visible as a health line, but a hand start is the only recourse.; Sample sizes: 3 Zoom calls, 0 browser mic rows, 22 h of the newest build. Zero errors in 60 release edges still allows a true error rate of about 5 percent.; A macOS release that changes process-object behaviour breaks the only primary signal. The shadow rows and the health line make that loud; nothing makes it impossible.

**Targets.** 100 percent is not attainable, for four reasons that no amount of engineering inside this app removes: macOS has no public statement that a call is live or which tab holds the microphone; the mic level is close to necessary but not sufficient (listen-only calls never raise it, some apps drop it on mute or hold, some keep it after the call); the vendors change their clients without notice, which is the whole history of END9 to END17; and consent is a human step unless the owner removes it. The competitors do not have it either: the research in this run found prompts by default, manual stop documented as the reliable end, and a steady stream of detection fixes at the one vendor that publishes them. What this design can honestly aim for on this laptop, each number to be confirmed by the shadow stage before it is claimed: Teams native, start: at least 98 percent of mic sessions of 60 s or more offered within 5 s of the microphone opening, and exactly one offer per session. Recorded share is then a consent question: with always-record, at least 98 percent recorded from within 5 s; with a click, today's unanswered offers (inherited: 30 of the 102 native prompts that stayed up a second or more) become late starts instead of lost calls. Teams native, end: no more than 1 false end per 100 recordings at first, and the content cut within 2 s of the release in at least 95 percent, with the file finalized 30 s later. The pack supports this (0 misjudged in 60 release edges, with the realness of 58 of them inherited and the gaps of eras A and B censored about a second after each release) but zero in 60 only bounds the true rate below about 5 percent; reaching a defensible 'under 1 percent' takes about 300 clean ends, four to five months of this owner's calls. Missed and late ends on Teams and Zoom: zero overruns beyond 35 s after a release, because the three in the pack (562 s, 63 s, 380 s) all had the release in the log. Zoom: the same targets with low confidence until 20 calls and bar Z1 are in. Google Meet in a browser: no target until bars B1 to B3 are measured. If they pass, the Teams targets minus naming (some offers will read 'Call in Chrome?'); if B2 or B3 fail, starts are caught but ends are owner-confirmed or about 2 minutes late; if B1 fails, this design does not solve browser Meet and the tab-URL or extension route is the next decision. Everything else (Webex, Slack, unlisted apps): offered, never promised.

**Effort.** About three to four working weeks of implementation spread over roughly eight calendar weeks, because three steps wait on field soak. Steps 0 to 3 (attribution, the shadow monitor, the reducer with its corpus, shadow decisions) are about one week of work and change no behaviour; they then soak for 10 working days. Steps 4 to 6 (end switch, start switch, the consent decision) are about one week plus two weeks of daily use behind flags. Step 7 (browsers) is up to a week and starts only if its probe passes. Steps 8 to 10 are a few days and are mostly deletion: the end state removes PromotionEngine's tiers, the hold and oracle, five signal classes, two adapters, the scorer gate and four suppression clocks, and adds one monitor and one reducer. Owner time: two scripted five-minute calls (one Meet in Chrome, one Zoom), one consent decision, and a weekly glance at the report.

### Call Belief: a level-sampled presence engine with microphone capture as its spine (fusion-done-right)

Today the app asks third-party UI whether a call is on, hears the answers as one-off edges, and lets whichever edge arrives first act. Replace that with one sampled picture. Every signal is a level with a read time; a stale level, or a 'gone' from a signal that was never 'present' in this call, counts as unknown. A pure engine holds an explicit belief about the call (absent, suspected, live, doubt, closed) with hysteresis in both directions. The spine of the belief is the one reading that was right on the work laptop every time it could be checked: whether the meeting app's own processes hold a microphone. Re-run on the pack: that flag was readable at the stop of all 69 native recordings, read true within 10 s of the start of all 69 (63 at the start itself), and read false for 1.63 percent of 30.41 recorded hours, 62 percent of that inside four recordings, mostly their post-call tails. UI readings (Leave control, window and tab titles) become identity and tie-breakers; alone they can neither start nor end a recording. Stopping, cutting and asking are separated: the recorder stops only after hysteresis, the cut is anchored at the last fresh sign of life and never at the first doubt, and when fresh evidence conflicts the app asks. Consent is bound to a call session, so one call gets one offer and it cannot expire into Skip while the call is up. Kept: the pure decide pattern, the per-app adapters, the HAL process-object matcher, the fresh accessibility re-walk, the Teams teardown parser, non-destructive trim with Restore, resume with a linked recording, and the replay corpus. Replaced: edge delivery, the phase engine, the first-stamp cut, the bundle-keyed cooldown, latch and resume, the dead mic-busy tier, and the 30 s prompt that turns into Skip. Browser Meet is the honest gap: the pack holds 0 browser capture readings, so every browser rule here is gated on bars measured in shadow first. Numbers marked re-run come from scripts I ran over the pack, kept in the session scratchpad ; numbers marked supplied come from this run's earlier field checks and were not re-run by me. Build eras, re-pinned: A is ae035d58 (END12, before END13's 04ffc40c) up to the rebuild at 2026-10-12T08:24:58Z; B is 8d4a9449 (END14, committed 19 min before that rebuild); C is f2d65038 (committed 83 s before the rebuild at 2026-11-12T09:19:44Z, carries PERF18's mute_poller_stopped), 35 commits behind HEAD 4949c9c0, and the only detection files that differ from HEAD are MeetingSessionController (40 lines) and LogMuteAdapter (a parsing speed change).

**State machine.** INPUTS. One sampler per signal. Each produces a sample on every tick whether or not anything changed: value (up, down, unknown) and asOf (when the read succeeded). A failed read yields unknown and does not advance asOf. From samples the engine keeps one Level per signal: value, since (first sample of the current run), run (consecutive samples with this value), everUp (has read up in this session). Two guards run before any rule sees a level. Freshness: if now minus asOf exceeds the signal's window the level is unknown (capture 3 s, window list 5 s, accessibility controls 15 s, power assertion 5 s, sound 3 s). Down needs a prior up: a signal that has never read up in this session reads unknown, so a first reading of 'gone' is not evidence.

CLASSES. Signals reduce to evidence classes: CAPTURE, CONTROLS, WINDOW, POWER, SOUND (positive only), plus DEFINITIVE events (app quit, the tab with this call's address closed, Teams' teardown line for this call id, owner Stop). A class is up if any member is up, down only if every member that has been up is down, otherwise unknown. Each app has a profile saying whether CAPTURE is trusted (Teams yes; Zoom provisionally; browsers only after their bars pass; Webex and Slack no).

BELIEF, one per call session: absent, suspected(since), live(since), doubt(since, lead, downAt), closed(at, cut, route). CallBelief.decide(belief, profile, snapshot, now) is pure and returns the next belief plus effects. It runs on every tick (1 Hz, and at once on a HAL listener callback) and on every owner action, in every recorder state. Nothing is delivered as an edge, so nothing can be dropped, and closed belongs to the session: the next evidence opens a new session, so the engine has no terminal state.

TRANSITIONS.
1. absent to suspected: any class reads up. suspected to absent: nothing up for 10 s.
2. suspected to live (a level must be held, never one sample): trusted CAPTURE up for 3 s and 3 samples; or, where capture is not trusted or cannot be read, two different weak classes up for 5 s. A browser additionally needs an identity (a Meet address, room code or Meet-shaped title in any window or tab of that browser). A browser with capture up and no identity stays suspected.
3. live to doubt: with trusted capture, only CAPTURE down for 5 s opens doubt. CONTROLS or WINDOW going down while capture is fresh and up changes nothing and stamps nothing (logged as weak_down_ignored). If capture is unknown for 10 s the session falls back to the weak rule and the HUD says detection is limited. Weak rule: every weak class that has been up is down for 10 s.
4. doubt to live: capture up for 2 samples with no new identity hash; or, in a weak session, a class that led the doubt is up again for 3 s; or the owner presses Still in the call, which pins live until capture has next gone up and down again (10 min in a weak session). A weak class coming back never reverts a capture-led doubt and never resets its clock.
5. doubt to closed: a DEFINITIVE event closes at once. Otherwise, capture session: capture down 45 s with no other class up closes. If another class is up from a sample taken after capture went down, the session is contested: the HUD asks, and from then on it follows the weak rules (it closes when that class has also been down 10 s, or after 120 s with no speech, or at the lone dwell). Weak session: closes at 120 s when two classes agree, at the lone dwell (12 min, the owner's 2026-10-24 decision) when only one class exists, and never while SOUND is up. Capture returning with a window-title hash this session has not seen closes the old session at its capture-down stamp and opens a new one. When nothing is being recorded, doubt closes after 10 s, because a wrong close there only costs a re-offer.
6. Precedence, one table for both directions: owner action, then DEFINITIVE, then trusted CAPTURE, then weak classes. A weak 'up' cannot revert a capture-led doubt or reset its clock (the Zoom shape of 2026-11-12); it can only make the close contested, which slows it and moves the cut later. A weak 'down' cannot overrule capture up (the Leave-absent shape). In both mic-switch traces the Leave control went down within seconds of capture, so a second class does not separate a brief drop from a real end; time and identity do. The second class exists for apps without trusted capture, for flagging a contested close, and for identity.

CUT. closed carries cut: the last moment something fresh said the call was alive. Capture-led close that nothing contradicted: the capture-down stamp. Teams teardown line present: the line's own time. Weak or contested close: the later of the last sample that read up and the last speech on either channel, plus 2 s. Never the moment doubt opened, and never earlier than a fresh reading that said the call was alive. Default posture keeps the audio and trims the transcript (existing Restore). Regulated or NDA truncates at the same cut, which is now anchored on liveness, so the first-stamp loss in audit finding G001 cannot occur.

SESSION REDUCER, also pure, replacing the controller's separate phase fields: idle, offered(session), starting(session), recording(session or none), closing(session, cut), finalizing. Events: belief changed, Record, Skip, Stop, recorder started or failed, recorder stopped. Rules: live while idle gives offered; the offer panel collapses to a chip after 30 s and never expires while belief is live; Skip marks this session declined until it closes; closed while offered withdraws the offer; Record or automatic consent gives starting then recording, and an end that arrives during starting is queued and applied when the recorder is up; a hand start binds to the live session if there is one, otherwise it records unbound and adopts the first session that goes live; doubt while recording shows the countdown and changes nothing else; closed while recording stops the recorder with the cut; only the bound session can stop a recording; for 180 s after a close the same app returning with no new identity hash resumes without a prompt into a linked recording, and a new hash is a new session with its own offer. The same inheritance holds for a refusal: a session that reopens inside 180 s with no new identity hash stays declined, so a skipped call is not offered again after a brief mic drop. Every start and stop writes its cause.

IDENTITY. The token is the set of salted window-title hashes seen during the session. Titles churn inside a call (the pack shows 2 to 10 distinct hashes in the 30 min before a gap, and the visible set differs right before and right after the 23.1 s same-call drop), so the test is 'a hash never seen in this session', not 'the set changed'. A new hash at a capture return gives a new session with its own offer and its own workflow routing, which avoids recording the next call unasked under the previous call's workflow (audit F046) at the price of a second offer if the split was wrong.

CONSTANTS, all in one BeliefProfile per app and all measured again in shadow before they act: live after 3 s and 3 samples; doubt after 5 s; revert after 2 samples; close at 45 s, contested close after 120 s without speech, weak pair at 120 s, lone class at 720 s; close with no recording running at 10 s; resume and inheritance window 180 s; speech guard 30 s; cut pad 2 s.

**When unsure.** Four states, each visible, none modal.

1. Unsure whether a call started (a browser or an unlisted app holds the mic and nothing identifies a meeting, or a weak-only app shows one class). The menu bar icon gains a dot and the top menu row reads '<App> is using the microphone' with Record beside it. No panel, no sound, no timeout, never an automatic recording. It goes away when the holder releases the mic. Audio: nothing is captured.

2. Sure a call is up, no answer yet. The panel shows for 30 s, then collapses to a chip reading 'Call detected' with Record, for as long as the belief is live. No answer never becomes Skip. Audio: nothing is captured unless the owner has chosen automatic recording for that app.

3. Unsure whether a call ended (doubt). The recorder keeps running. The HUD changes from Recording to 'Call may have ended, saving in 0:40' with Keep recording and End now. If the app takes the mic back it returns to Recording without a sound and nothing is marked. If fresh evidence conflicts (mic released, call controls still found) it reads '<App> released the microphone but the call window is still open' with End here and Still in the call, and waits under the weak rules (until the controls are gone too, or 120 s without speech, or the lone dwell). When it closes, the transcript is cut at the last fresh sign of life, which in a contested close is the last reading or speech that said alive, not the mic release; in the default posture the audio after the cut stays on disk with Restore; under regulated or NDA the same cut truncates.

4. The detector cannot see (a sampler has gone silent, capture cannot be read, Accessibility or Screen Recording was lost). The HUD and the menu say 'Detection limited' and name what cannot be read, ends fall back to asking first, and nothing is inferred from the silence.

**Still fails when:** Two calls in the same app less than 45 s apart, when the window titles give no new hash, fuse into one recording. Three such returns (30.9, 32.3, 43.8 s) are in the pack; the identity rule separates the one that has title rows, and the rule itself rests on 10 gaps.; A real in-call drop or hold longer than 45 s is closed and resumed as a second, linked recording.; An app that keeps the mic after the call (Webex by design; Zoom and others unmeasured) ends late, on weak classes, a question, or the idle ceiling.; A call joined without opening the mic never raises capture. Natives fall back to two weak classes; a browser gets no offer. The pack has 0 such native recordings in 69.; Browser Meet rests on unmeasured behaviour: which process captures, whether Meet releases capture on mute, what the title and address look like after leaving. Any bar can fail, and then that browser is a weak session with questions and late ends.; A Meet tab that is not the selected tab, without Automation consent, only ever gets the unsure chip. Firefox is selected-tab only.; Dictation, voice search or a second web call in a browser that also shows a Meet tab can raise one false offer per session.; A vendor update can rename the helper process or change when it holds the mic. The canary is cap_attribution_lost; until someone reacts the app runs on weak rules and says so.; An offer nobody clicks is still a meeting not recorded, unless the owner turns on automatic recording for that app.; Window titles churn inside a call (seen in the pack), so the identity test is 'a hash never seen in this session'. How often a never-seen hash shows up at a capture return inside one call, which would split it and raise a second offer, is unknown; it is counted in shadow before the rule may act.; A daemon relaunch mid-call still splits the meeting: the session is rebuilt from levels within seconds, but the first half is a separate, linked recording.; A HAL listener or process-object churn can make capture unknown for a few seconds; under 10 s it is ridden out, longer degrades the session to weak rules.; Under regulated or NDA a weak close still truncates, at the last speech; if speech detection misses quiet speech, that tail is lost for good. Capture-led cuts do not have this risk.; New engine, new bugs. Shadow mode and the pack replay reduce this; they do not remove it.

**Targets.** The honest answer to '100 percent, like competitors': no competitor is at 100 percent and the careful ones say so. The vendor that sells detection to other recorders published fixes through 2026 for missed, phantom, early-closed and never-closed meetings, including Meet in a background tab; one popular notes app documents that back-to-back calls can merge and that pressing stop is the most reliable end; another labels its detection beta; the open-source trackers show the same misses. macOS offers no public call-is-live signal, so every outside recorder infers. What competitors do have, and this app lacks, is the cheap signal they nearly all lean on (a known app's process holding the mic) and a habit of asking when unsure. This design adopts both. What is attainable by construction: never stop a Teams or Zoom recording while that app holds the mic; never cut before the last fresh sign of life; never let one UI reading start or end anything; one offer per call. What the data can and cannot show: the capture flag was readable and consistent at 69 of 69 native recordings, which bounds the flag's own failure rate only below about 4 percent (rule of three) and says nothing yet about the engine built on it. The replay is in-sample: the 45 s close and the hash rule were chosen after looking at these same gaps, and the replay still shows 1 split and at least 3 ends where the next capture came back inside 45 s. Only shadow weeks are out of sample, and a quarter of roughly 150 calls can bound a rate near 2 percent, not below. Targets per quarter on the work laptop, Teams native: detection-made early stops 0 observed; an offer within 10 s of capture-up on at least 97 percent of calls of 2 min or more; closed within 60 s of mic release on at least 95 percent, with the cut within 5 s of release; offers per call at most 1.1 (the pack: 407 prompts on 97 spans); mute contradictions over 5 s in at most 5 percent of recordings (the pack: 15 of 66). Zoom: same rules, too few calls to quote a rate. Chrome Meet: no number until the bars are measured; if they pass, an offer on at least 90 percent of calls whose tab identity is readable. Safari and Firefox: best effort, with the hotkey as the stated fallback. Recorded share is a consent choice, not a detector property: with a prompt, 53 of 89 Teams calls of 2 min or more began recording within 30 s in the pack; the non-expiring offer removes the expired-prompt class (19 of the 22 unrecorded spans), and only automatic recording for Teams gets near every call.

**Effort.** About 4 to 5 working weeks of implementation spread over 9 to 11 calendar weeks, because three steps wait on soak time with real meetings. Step 1 half a day; step 2 two days plus a week of logging; step 3 two days; step 4 three days; step 5 five days plus two weeks of shadow; step 6 three days plus two weeks of reverse shadow; step 7 four days; step 8 three days, in parallel; step 9 four days plus ten real Meet calls; step 10 two days. The first owner-visible gains arrive early and do not wait for the engine: step 3 (the offer that cannot expire) addresses the largest start-side loss in the pack, and step 2 answers the browser questions with ordinary calls instead of owner-run probes. Owner time: one scripted test call for step 6, one look at the chip for step 3, and the decisions listed under risks.

### Capture first, segment after: open and close on the microphone, cut from the journal (never-lose-audio)

Today one real-time decision answers three questions at once (is a call up, when did it end, may the file be cut), and it answers them from proxies a third-party UI controls: a Leave button, a window title, a tab title. Every END task from END9 to END17 moved the same error between "too early" and "too late". This design splits the three questions and never lets the weak ones touch the recorder.

1. Capture. A session opens as soon as a call is plausibly up and keeps recording until a strong cause stops it. The primary evidence is not UI: it is the call app's own process holding the microphone (the per-process CoreAudio flag the repo already reads for Teams and Zoom), promoted from a poll-time tiebreaker to an always-on service. Leave buttons, windows and titles become hints in a journal. They never stop a recording and never place a cut.

2. Segmentation. After the stop, a pure function over a 1 Hz evidence journal plus the recording's own channel levels places the start and end marks. The mark is the last evidence of life (mic release, a call-bound teardown line, last activity), never the first doubt. The audio is untouched in lossless modes, so a wrong mark is one click from undone.

3. Presentation. When the detector is unsure the owner sees it on the HUD with two buttons (Still in call, Finish now) instead of having it acted on.

Why the microphone, from the work-laptop pack (my scripts, definitions in the measurement field): in 62 of 69 native recordings the app's mic flag made a final true to false edge before the stop, and the last transcribed segment ends a median 1.7 s before that edge (p90 6.1 s, n=61); only 2 recordings carry more than 2 s of transcribed speech after it (2.2 and 2.3 s). The other 7 are the four chopped recordings of 2026-10-08 and 2026-10-09 (mic still live at the stop, so this rule would not have stopped them) and three stops with no end row before them (hand stops by inference). On the start side, 28 of the 30 Teams prompts that timed out into Skip had the app's mic flag true while the prompt was up, and 311 of the 313 prompts that vanished within 1.5 s did too. The per-process read stayed resolvable through all 69 native recordings (0 with more than 1 s unresolved while the last reading was true).

What it does not solve, said plainly: browsers are unmeasured (the pack has no mic reading for Chrome because browsers are excluded from the read), so the browser half is gated on four owner-run probes with bars fixed in advance; mic release is trusted as an end only for apps proven to keep the mic while muted (Teams today); a call that never holds the mic and shows no readable UI is still only catchable by hand.

On "100 percent, like competitors": no botless recorder reaches it or claims it. The research in this run shows the vendor that sells detection to others shipping fixes for missed, phantom, early-closed and never-closed meetings in almost every release of 2026, and the best-known note taker calling its Stop button the most reliable end. Most of them prompt rather than auto-start and lean on the same microphone signal plus a calendar this owner's Mac cannot supply (CAL1). What is attainable here is different and better than a number: no silent loss. Failures become extra audio instead of missing audio, every uncertain decision is visible, and every cut is reversible for a stated period.

Six decisions belong to the owner and are flagged where they arise: capturing before consent with an Unconfirmed shelf; a sealed short-lived tail vault under regulated and NDA (this amends ADR 0016 and ADR 0017, which today allow no post-call audio at rest); an expiry for kept tails in normal modes; any new grant on the work laptop (Automation per browser, a private call for Safari attribution, a browser extension); a RAM-only pre-roll under the strict posture; and the 60 s close dwell.

**State machine.** Three parts, all pure decisions in MeetingPipeCore with hosts that only collect inputs: an always-on holder table, a session reducer, and a segmenter that runs after the stop.

A. InputHolders (always on)
Table keyed by HAL object id: pid, bundle id, isRunningInput. A family is one registry app (the [native], [mic_plausible] and browser bundle lists in meeting_apps.toml) plus every object the existing matcher attributes to it. Output per family, debounced: holds = live after 3 s continuously true (5 s for a browser family); released after 3 s continuously false; unknown when the list will not read or the matched objects vanish while the last reading was live. Unknown is never held as live. Every transition writes a title-free row (family, pid, state, reason listener or poll).

B. Session reducer: decide(state, event, now) returns the next state and a list of effects
Identity = family + mic-hold span id (+ Teams call id or Meet room when readable). Every suppression, resume and consent decision keys on the identity, never on a bundle id plus a wall clock.

IDLE
- On holder live (debounced), or a confident legacy scan, or the owner's I am in a meeting: open a session. If this identity was declined earlier, stay idle and write session_suppressed (one row per suppressed sighting, so a miss can be counted).
- Effects on open: resolve workflow and capture mode, fail closed (see strict posture below); start the recorder; start the journal; show the chip or the HUD.

OPEN.PROVISIONAL (consent pending; skipped when the app is on Always or the start was manual)
- Recording runs into a quarantine location outside the Library, with a provisional marker file beside the intermediates.
- Keep: go to OPEN.CONFIRMED, move nothing yet, remove the marker at close.
- Discard or Not a meeting: stop, delete the audio at once, mark the identity declined. The decline clears when that span has been released for 30 s.
- Blip: if the holder releases within 20 s of opening and the system channel never rose above its floor, discard silently and log it.
- No timer turns silence into Skip. The chip stays for the life of the span. All stop rules below apply; a session that closes unanswered goes to the Unconfirmed shelf.
- Strict posture (regulated flag on, or any NDA workflow could match this source: a bundle rule matches, or a title rule exists and the title is unreadable): nothing is written before Keep. A later switch to an NDA workflow on a session that began lossless applies the strict posture from that instant, redacts the provisional span from the recorded mute timeline, and says so in the sidecar.

OPEN.CONFIRMED, with a presence sub-state recomputed every second from the last samples
- LIVE: the holder is live; or the holder is unknown or untrusted and either channel was above its floor in the last 120 s.
- POSSIBLE_END(since, why): the holder is released (since = the release time); or the holder is unknown or untrusted and both channels have been below floor for 120 s (since = last active second + 5 s).
- POSSIBLE_END returns to LIVE when the same family holds the mic again, or the owner presses Still in call. Still in call also marks the holder untrusted for the rest of the session, so only the silence rules can end it, and writes a ground-truth label.

Stop causes. Each is written on recording_stopped as stop_cause, with the last journal row.
1. user_stop: now.
2. app_exit: the family's main pid exited. Now.
3. teardown_bound: an unregister line whose call id was registered inside this session, and the holder is released: 10 s after the line's own timestamp. If the holder is still live, ignore the line and log the contradiction.
4. quiet_close, only for apps on the release allow-list (Teams today; each other app after its mute probe): holder released continuously for 60 s and the system channel below floor for at least 20 of the last 30 s. If system audio is still active, extend: the HUD says so, and the stop comes 600 s after the release regardless.
5. silence_close, for an unknown or untrusted holder, or a released holder that is not on the allow-list: both channels below floor, nudge at 300 s, stop at 600 s.
6. live_silence: holder live but both channels below floor: nudge at 900 s, stop at 2,700 s. There is no stand-down for a lifecycle that reads live, because no lifecycle verdict is consulted.
7. sleep: at willSleep.
8. max_length: nudge at 3 h 45 min with a Keep going 1 h button, stop at 4 h (configurable).
9. low_space: the existing REC20 stop.
10. superseded: only while POSSIBLE_END is open (the holder has already released) and a different family passes its own open dwell: stop the old session now, open the new one. A second app taking the mic while the first still holds it changes nothing and is only logged.
Never a stop cause: Leave control invalid, window gone, tab or window title change, title mismatch, an accessibility blackout, an unreadable log, a mute state, or any event from an adapter that does not belong to this session.

CLOSING
recorder.stop with the handle that has been open the whole time (REC10: a finalized capture file is never reopened); run the segmenter; write marks, stop_cause and end_confidence to the meta sidecar; enqueue the pipeline (confirmed) or shelve (unconfirmed); enter REOPEN_WATCH.

REOPEN_WATCH(identity, 300 s)
The same family holds the mic again: start a new recording at once with the inherited consent and workflow, into a new stem carrying resumed_from, and the Library offers Merge (suggested by default when the gap is under 120 s). A different family: a normal open. Expiry: IDLE. A relaunch finds the journal of a session under 5 minutes old and applies the same rule, so a rebuild mid-call does not re-prompt.

Timers and hysteresis, with what sized them
- Open dwell 3 s, release debounce 3 s: the pack's start-of-recording flaps are 0.4, 0.5 and 1.5 s.
- Close dwell 60 s: the two same-call re-holds measured end to end are 23.1 s (2026-11-12, held and reverted) and 23.5 s (2026-10-22, the mic switch). Across all 67 release edges, the next sighting of the flag true came within 30 s on 4, in 30 to 60 s on 4, in 60 to 120 s on 3, in 120 to 300 s on 3, and not within 15 minutes on 53; those sightings depend on scan cadence, so they are coarse, and whether the 30 to 300 s ones were the same call or the next call cannot be told from the log. Step 1 of the migration measures this directly before the dwell is trusted.
- Reopen watch 300 s: covers every re-hold in that table.
- Extend cap 600 s; silence 300 and 600 s; live silence 900 and 2,700 s; teardown 10 s; decline clears 30 s after release.

C. Segmenter (pure, after the stop)
End mark by cause: user_stop uses the open POSSIBLE_END's since when the owner pressed Finish now, else now. teardown_bound uses the line's timestamp. app_exit uses the earlier of exit and release. quiet_close uses the release time plus 2 s. silence_close uses the last active second plus 5 s and is labelled estimated. live_silence, sleep, max_length and low_space trim nothing unless a POSSIBLE_END was open. A weak-signal time is never a mark.
Head mark: a leading stretch with both channels below floor for more than 20 s is trimmed to 5 s before first activity, labelled estimated.
Gap markers: a release of 10 s or more that came back is recorded as a marker, not a cut.
Output keys: held_end_trim_sec (the existing key, re-anchored), head_trim_sec, end_confidence (exact, high, estimated), stop_cause. The mark is computed from the journal on every stop route, so no route can skip it and nothing lives in controller memory.

What happens to the tail
- Lossless modes: the WAV keeps the tail; transcript, summary and every sink stop at the mark (the existing HeldEndTrim read); Restore is one click (existing). New, owner decision: after tail_retention_days (proposed 7) a reaper truncates at mark + 10 s.
- Strict posture, owner decision that amends ADR 0016 and 0017: today the tail is cut at stop at the hold's first stamp, which destroys meeting audio when that stamp was a Leave flicker (G001). Proposed: the cut is anchored at the mark above, and the tail moves to a sealed vault outside the Library (mode 0600, excluded from backup and cloud sync) for a TTL the owner sets, proposed 24 h, then is destroyed. TTL 0 keeps today's no-audio-at-rest rule, cutting only on exact or high marks with a 30 s pad. That is what "restorable in every mode" can honestly mean: restorable until the TTL the owner chose.

**When unsure.** Nothing is stopped, cut or discarded on uncertainty. The owner sees one of five states, and the audio keeps flowing in all of them. Start unsure. A chip, not a 30 s modal: "Teams call, recording provisionally. Keep / Discard". It stays as long as the app holds the mic. Unanswered, the recording is kept on the Unconfirmed shelf (not transcribed, not summarized, not published), with a notification at the end of the call and a Library banner: Keep or Discard, auto-deleted after the TTL (proposed 72 h). For a mic hold with no meeting evidence (a browser with no Meet title, an unlisted app) the chip asks and nothing is recorded until answered. End unsure. The HUD line changes within about 5 s of the mic release: "Call seems over (Teams released the microphone at 14:32:10). Finishing in 0:47. Still in call / Finish now". If the mic comes back it silently returns to Recording. If other audio is still playing it says so and gives the 10 minute bound. The recording runs through the countdown; the mark is already at the release, so the transcript will not contain the wait. Holder unknown. "Recording. I cannot see the call app, so I will stop after 10 minutes of silence. Meeting over". This is the state of a browser call before the probes pass, and of a hand-started recording that found no app to adopt. Blind. A small badge with the reason when an input is not reading (process list unreadable, Accessibility off, Screen Recording off, Teams log unreadable). A signal that has never produced a sample in the session shows as never read, in the HUD tooltip and in doctor, so a dead input cannot look like a quiet one. After the fact. The Library row carries what was decided and how sure it was: "End estimated. Restore tail", "Unconfirmed. Keep / Discard", "Call dropped for 43 s here", "Resumed from the previous recording. Merge". Every one of those clicks is logged as a ground-truth label.

**Still fails when:** A call that never takes the microphone and shows no readable UI: a listen-only webinar in a background tab, a join with audio off. Only the manual control catches it.; A browser call when the probes fail for that browser: start depends on the Meet tab being the selected tab of an on-screen window, and the end is estimated from silence, up to 10 minutes late with an estimated mark.; An app that releases the microphone on mute. Guarded by the allow-list, but an app wrongly placed on it would have a muted live call stopped 60 s to 10 minutes after the mute; the reopen watch restarts it when the owner unmutes, and the gap is lost.; A real in-call release longer than 60 s (a long device renegotiation, a call drop) still splits the meeting in two with a Merge offer. The dwell rests on two end-to-end observations (23.1 and 23.5 s).; Back-to-back calls joined within the close dwell fuse into one recording with a gap marker until split support lands; telling same call from next call rests on a call id or room code that is often unreadable.; A holder-unknown recording with other audio playing for hours (music after a browser call) never reaches silence and runs to the 4 h cap. The estimated mark is then late too.; A quiet real meeting: holder live, nobody speaks for 45 minutes, is stopped after two nudges.; Provisional capture records calls the owner would have declined (5 or 6 explicit skips in the pack). The audio exists until Discard or the shelf TTL.; Short false holds longer than 20 s (a pre-join screen the owner backs out of, a mic test) create an Unconfirmed item or, under Always, a short recording.; The strict posture with TTL 0 still destroys audio on a wrong mute reading and cannot restore a tail. That is the owner's chosen trade, not a defect the design removes.; The legacy scan stays load-bearing before an app takes the mic (5 of 96 picks led the mic flag by 5 s or more, the longest 70.55 s), so its fragility remains for that head.; The daemon not running, relaunching mid-call beyond the 5 minute journal window, or missing Microphone or Screen Recording grants. Detection cannot see what the process cannot.; A vendor changing which process carries the audio. The OR across a family's objects absorbs most shapes; a move outside the bundle prefix and outside the pid ancestry (the Safari shape) would read as unknown, which degrades to the silence rules and a blind badge, not to a wrong stop.; Zoom, Webex and Slack ends remain silence-based and late until each passes its mute probe; Zoom has 3 calls in the pack.

**Targets.** 100 percent is not attainable, here or anywhere, for four structural reasons: macOS has no public API that says a call is live or which tab holds the microphone; every UI signal belongs to a vendor who can change it overnight (2026-10-08 is the proof); a call that takes no microphone and shows no readable UI leaves nothing to detect; and the recorder must be running and permitted. The competitors in this run's research do not reach it either; they prompt, they ask Keep recording when unsure, and they document the Stop button as the reliable path.

What this design can honestly aim for, by metric, with the exposure needed to claim it. The pack holds roughly 100 calls in 38 active days (brief's ledger), so zero failures in N calls only bounds the rate near 3 divided by N: about 5 percent after one month, 1 percent after about 300 calls, which is five to six months of the owner's use.

Native calls (Teams first, Zoom after its probe), app on Always, lossless posture:
- Capture: at least 99 percent of witnessed call-seconds for calls of 2 minutes or more. The pack's figure for a recording beginning within 30 s is 61 percent (54 of 88, brief).
- Missed start (no audio for more than the first 30 s of a witnessed call): at most 1 in 50 in the first month, then tightened on data.
- Recording stopped while the call is still up: target zero; claimable bound as above. The pack's rate is 7 of 74.
- End mark within 10 s of the real end: at least 95 percent. Basis: the release sits within 6.1 s of the last transcribed speech at p90 over 61 recordings.
- Stop latency: about 60 s after the end by design, 10 s when a bound teardown line is readable; the HUD acknowledges the end within about 5 s. This is a stated cost, not a defect.
- Runaway: no recording runs more than 10 minutes past a release, more than 45 minutes of two-channel silence, or past the length cap.
- Stops with no logged cause: zero. Chips per call: at most one, zero under Always.

Browser Meet: no numeric target until the probes have run. Expected order of reliability is Chromium, then Safari, then Firefox. With no probe passing, the floor is: a Meet whose tab is selected and on screen is admitted (the five missed episodes in the pack would have been), a background-tab Meet is not, and the end is estimated from silence.

Mute: advisory. Target under 1 percent of Teams recording time in contradiction with the live button, from 4.9 percent (brief). No target for Zoom or browsers; the honest state there is unknown.

Strict posture: the same capture and mark targets; restorability is bounded by the TTL the owner sets and is zero at TTL 0.

The claim to hold the design to is not a percentage but a property: no silent loss. A failure costs extra audio, a visible label and a click, never a missing meeting that nobody was told about.

**Effort.** About 30 working days of focused implementation across 13 steps, none larger than one week, plus about 1 hour of owner time for the probes and two 2-week shadow windows in normal use. In calendar terms roughly two months to the full design. Front-loaded relief: step 0 is a setting the owner can change today; steps 1, 2 and 4 are about 5 days together and remove the causeless stops, the wrong cut anchor, the skipped trim and the unbounded overrun, which are the failures still live in the newest build. Steps 5 to 7 (about 10 days) remove the prompt as a failure point and start recording on the microphone. Step 8 (about 1 week, flag-gated) moves the stop authority. Steps 9 to 12 (about 10 to 12 days) are the manual controls, the browser branches the probes allow, the mute repairs, the strict-posture decision and the deletion of retired code. No new third-party dependency. New grants are optional, listed, and each needs the owner's explicit yes: Automation per browser, a private call for Safari attribution, a browser extension. Owner decisions needed before the steps that depend on them: capture before consent and the shelf TTL (step 7); the close dwell after step 2's measurement (step 8); browser grants (step 10); the vault and tail expiry (step 12).
