# REC20: a recording starts with no free-space check and is never watched for low space, transcription loads the whole meeting into the recorder's process, and the library only ever grows unless a workflow opts in

Band origin: filed 2026-10-24 from the 2026-10-24 audit band ([go-live assessment](../../audits/2026-10-21-go-live-assessment.md) PD-13). Takes over the "Transcription RAM peak (2026-07-12)" item from the watch list in [meetingpipe-q6-backlog.md](../meetingpipe-q6-backlog.md). Status and priority live in this file's header; [INDEX.md](../INDEX.md) lists every task.

## Context

Three unbounded resources, verified in code on 2026-10-24.

**Disk during a recording.** The intermediates grow at a fixed rate: the system channel at 384,000 bytes a second (`SystemAudioCapture.captureFormat`, 48 kHz stereo Float32) and the mic at 192,000 bytes a second at a 48 kHz device (mono Float32), about 2.07 GB an hour together. The stop-time merge then writes the 16 kHz stereo final (64,000 bytes a second) while both intermediates still exist. `grep -rn "volumeAvailableCapacity\|statfs"` over `daemon/Sources` finds one reader, `StorageStats` (`volumeAvailableCapacityForImportantUsageKey`), which feeds the Preferences ▸ Storage free-space row and nothing else. Nothing checks free space when a recording starts or while it runs. The only defence is REC3's write-failure force-stop (`WriteFailureTracker`), which fires after 32 consecutive failed writes, by which time the whole Mac is out of space, and whose notification REC19 is correcting.

**Memory during transcription.** `FluidAudioRunner.transcribe` calls `readMonoFloat32(from:)`, which allocates one `AVAudioPCMBuffer` for the entire file (`frameCapacity: AVAudioFrameCount(file.length)`), mixes it to a mono `[Float]`, resamples to 16 kHz, and holds that array across ASR and diarization. TECH-PERF2 scoped the full-clip buffer so it is freed once the mono array exists, which halved the peak; the watch-list note measured "about 2 GB peak for a 3 h recording". This runs inside the daemon, the same process that records, and back-to-back meetings mean meeting N transcribes while meeting N+1 records. The diarizer is the reason the read is whole-file: `FluidAudioRunner`'s doc comment says driving `DiarizerManager.performCompleteDiarization` chunk by chunk "would re-implement its speaker tracking". Nothing records the real peak: no `phys_footprint` or RSS reading exists anywhere in `daemon/Sources`. `launchctl print` on the owner's Mac shows `jetsam memory limit (active) = (unlimited)` for the job, so the risk is memory pressure and swap slowing the live recording, not a kill.

**The library over time.** STOR1's retention is per workflow and opt-in: `WorkflowRetention.policy` defaults to `.keep`, `AudioRetentionSweep.sweep` returns at once when every policy is `.keep` ("Every workflow on keep-forever is the default state"), and `AudioRetention.decide` skips any meeting with no `workflowID`. That default is the owner's (STOR1) and this task keeps it; what is missing is any surface that says where the growth is heading.

## Scope

1. **Preflight at record start.** Before `MeetingRecorder` opens its files, read `volumeAvailableCapacityForImportantUsage` for the output directory. Below the space for two hours of intermediates plus their merge (computed from the two capture rates above, documented beside the constant), still record, since a meeting is never refused, but show a HUD notice naming the free space and the hours it covers, and emit `recording_low_space_at_start` with `free_bytes` and `hours_covered`.
2. **Watch while recording.** Re-read free space every 60 s on the recorder's existing timer or a utility queue (never main). When the remaining space covers less than 15 minutes plus the merge, notify once, and when it covers less than the merge alone, stop the recording cleanly through the normal stop path, which merges, rather than waiting for the write-failure force-stop. Emit `recording_low_space` and `recording_stopped_low_space`.
3. **Measure transcription memory, then decide.** Emit the process `phys_footprint` (from `task_info(TASK_VM_INFO)`) at the start and peak of each transcription as `transcription_memory` with `audio_sec`, and record a 2 h and a 3 h meeting's numbers in this file. If the peak exceeds a threshold the pickup states (about 25% of physical memory) or a live recording overlapped a transcription with a measurable stall, the follow-up is to defer a transcription while a recording is live and memory is tight, not to chunk the diarizer; write that decision here with the numbers.
4. **Make growth visible.** The Preferences ▸ Storage pane gains one line from data it already has: the library's growth over the last 30 days and, at that rate, how long the free space lasts. `mp doctor` warns when that is under 30 days. Meetings with no `workflowID` follow the default workflow's retention (`Workflow.isDefault`), so turning retention on for the default actually covers them.

## Explicitly not

- Changing the keep-forever default. STOR1 made it the owner's per-workflow choice, and audio loss by default is worse than disk growth.
- Windowed ASR or a chunked diarizer. That moves DIAR2's tuned output (FluidAudioRunner's doc comment) and is not justified until item 3 measures a problem; LOCAL18 owns the separate free-RAM watchdog for the local summarizer.
- Moving transcription out of the daemon into a child process. A real option if item 3 shows overlap stalls, recorded so it is not lost; it is an architecture change with its own task.
- The 4 GiB intermediate ceiling and the stop notification's wording: REC19.

## Acceptance

- Pure, unit-tested helpers (MeetingPipeTests): the hours-covered computation for given free bytes and capture rates; the three-band decision (fine, warn, stop) for a sequence of free-space readings, including that the warning fires once; the retention fallback, where a candidate with no `workflowID` takes the default workflow's policy and is untouched when the default is `.keep`; the growth projection over a fixture of meeting sizes and dates.
- A recorder test with an injected free-space reader that the stop band triggers the normal stop path (merge runs, `recording_stopped_low_space` emitted), not `onWriteFailure`.
- pytest for the doctor warning (projection under 30 days warns, over does not).
- `cd daemon && swift build` and `swift test`, `cd pipeline && uv run --extra dev pytest -q` green; `python3 scripts/truth_fences.py events` green for the new actions; new test counts in the commit body.
- This file gains a dated "Measured" section with item 3's footprint numbers before the task is closed.

## Amended 2026-10-25 (performance review)

### Where the 2 GB transcription peak comes from, a bit-identical block-wise read, and the post-job footprint of the never-released models

Findings SUBPROCESS-13 and SUBPROCESS-14 from the 2026-10-25 performance review (eight subsystem finders over HEAD `01e904a`, every finding adversarially re-verified, both confirmed). They put numbers on item 3's question and add a cheap leg that changes no output. Re-verified in code on 2026-10-25.

**1. Where the 2 GB comes from.** The stop-time final is 16 kHz stereo s16 (`RecordingPostProcessor`'s merge writes `-ar 16000 -c:a pcm_s16le` after `amerge`), so `FluidAudioRunner.readMonoFloat32` takes its no-resample branch. It allocates one `AVAudioPCMBuffer(pcmFormat: format, frameCapacity: frameCount)` with `frameCount = AVAudioFrameCount(file.length)` in `processingFormat` (deinterleaved Float32, two channels), reads the whole file into it, and `mixDownToMono` then builds a `[Float]` of the same length while that buffer is still alive. For 3 h that is 172.8M frames: 1.38 GB of stereo plus 0.69 GB of mono, about 2.07 GB at the peak, which matches the watch-list figure this task took over. TECH-PERF2's scoping frees the stereo buffer after the mix, not during it, so its comment ("Halves the readMonoFloat32 peak") is true only relative to the code before it. The 0.69 GB mono array then lives through ASR and diarization. Per recorded hour that is about 0.69 GB of peak and 0.23 GB held.

**2. A fix that does not touch the diarizer.** Read the file in fixed blocks (for example 30 s, one reused 480,000-frame buffer) and mix each block straight into a preallocated `[Float]` of `file.length` frames. The peak drops to the mono array plus one block, about 0.69 GB for 3 h, and the samples are bit-identical, so DIAR2's tuned output cannot move. This is not the windowed ASR or chunked diarizer this task's Explicitly not rules out: both engines still receive the whole mono array. It can land as item 3a without waiting for item 3's measurement, which then measures the new peak. Two constraints. A non-16 kHz source (an older file, or a re-transcribe of one) still needs the whole mono array for `resampleMono`, so either resample per block with one persistent `AVAudioConverter` or keep that path's peak and say so. And [DIAR6](./DIAR6.md)'s per-channel gain normalization needs each channel's voiced level before mixing, which a block reader gets from a first streaming pass over the file; whichever of the two lands second keeps the other's property.

**3. The models stay resident for the daemon's lifetime, and nobody has measured what that costs.** `FluidAudioRunner` caches `asrManager`, `diarizer` and `diarizerModels` on first use and never releases them; the cache is deliberate, to avoid CoreML recompilation (its doc comment). On disk the Parakeet TDT v3 model is 461 MB and the diarizer 13 MB (`~/Library/Application Support/FluidAudio/Models`, measured 2026-10-25). The installed daemon measured on 2026-10-25 (up 7 days 8 hours, 18 MB footprint) had not transcribed anything in that process's lifetime, so the resident cost after a meeting is unmeasured, and mapped weights are clean pages the system can evict, which may make it small. Add to item 3: also record `phys_footprint` 15 minutes after the last job; if the models hold a material share, release the three managers once the queue has been idle for a set time and rebuild them lazily (compiled models persist on disk, so measure the reload too), and write that decision here with the numbers.

#### Added acceptance

- `FluidAudioRunnerTests`: the block-wise `readMonoFloat32` returns samples bit-identical to the whole-file read on a fixture 16 kHz stereo WAV whose length is not a multiple of the block, and on a 44.1 kHz fixture through the resample path.
- The Measured section gains the peak for a 2 h and a 3 h meeting before and after item 3a, and the footprint 15 minutes after a job.

### Measured final rate, a wrong rate in Workflow.swift, the keep-default rationale does not cover lossless compress, and intermediates in the synced tree

Two findings from the 2026-10-25 performance review (RECORDING-8, confirmed medium; PERSISTENCE-14, confirmed low) confirm the library-growth half of this task. They add a measured rate, a correction to the code's own figure, a correction to this spec's reason for keeping the default, and one unfiled adjacency. Re-checked against `main` at `01e904a` on 2026-10-25.

**Measured rate.** The final recording is 64,000 bytes a second (16 kHz stereo `pcm_s16le`, `RecordingPostProcessor`'s ffmpeg argument list): the owner's `20261016-122748.wav` is 71,669,838 bytes for the 1,119.84 s its `intermediate_durations` event recorded, so about 230 MB per recorded hour kept. On 2026-10-25 the library holds 9 WAVs in 324 MB and no FLAC, and with every workflow on keep `AudioRetentionSweep` returns at once. At the review's assumed load (2 to 8 meetings a day, often an hour each) that is roughly 115 to 460 GB a year; at the owner's measured pace it is far less, which is why item 4's projection should use the library's own last 30 days rather than a constant.

**The code's figure is wrong.** `RetentionPolicy`'s doc comment in `Workflow.swift` says "Stereo WAV runs ~0.7 GB per recorded hour", about three times the real final rate and about a third of the 2.07 GB an hour the intermediates take while recording. Correct it in item 4's commit, and name both rates beside the constants item 1 adds, so the growth line and the preflight read one source.

**The reason given for keeping the default does not cover `.compress`.** Explicitly not keeps the keep-forever default because "audio loss by default is worse than disk growth". That holds for `.drop`. `.compress` loses nothing: STOR1 chose FLAC, lossless and about half the size, so that `soundfile` can still decode it, and `AudioTranscoder` verifies the duration before deleting the WAV. So whether the default workflow (and, through item 4's fallback, meetings with no workflow) should compress after N days is an owner question this spec has not asked, not one it has answered. The pickup asks once and records the dated answer here; the default does not change without it. If the answer is yes, the change waits until the retention sweep is single-flight (review finding PERSISTENCE-6 ([CONC5](./CONC5.md)), from the same review's storage-sweep findings), because the first sweep over a large backlog is exactly the overlapping-sweep case.

**Observed, not filed: the intermediates live in the synced tree.** `MeetingRecorder.start(outputDir:captureMode:voiceProcessing:)` creates `<stem>.mic.wav` and `<stem>.system.wav` in `outputDir`, the library's `raw/`, and `RecordingPostProcessor` writes `<stem>.merging.wav` beside them. On the owner's Mac `~/Documents` carries `com.apple.icloud.desktop` (xattr read 2026-10-25), so during every recording about 2 GB an hour of files that are deleted minutes after stop sit in an iCloud Desktop & Documents folder; whether iCloud uploads a file that is still being written cannot be measured from code. [STOR6](./STOR6.md) moves new installs out of `~/Documents` but pins existing libraries in place (its item 2), so this stays true for the owner until the library moves. Moving the intermediates to a non-synced directory is not a local change: `OrphanRecordingRecovery`, `OrphanScan`, `StrandedJobRecovery`, `LibraryMover` and `MeetingStore` all look for `.mic.wav` and `.system.wav` beside the final, and moving the writer without them makes a crash-orphaned recording invisible to recovery. If the owner keeps the library in iCloud, this deserves its own task, and item 1's free-space read must then target whichever directory holds the intermediates.

## Owner answers: the HUD countdown and the retroactive fallback (2026-11-12)

Two questions the pickup returned, both answered on 2026-11-12:

- **The HUD countdown is accepted as built.** It reuses the state label, shows time only ("Disk: 1h 25m left"; the notification carries the free space), and outranks "Off the record" (`HUDStateLabel`: lost mic, then low disk, then off the record, then confirming end).
- **The retroactive reach is accepted.** Meetings with no `workflow_id` follow the default workflow's policy, past ones included: if the default ever leaves keep-forever, the next sweep reaches every earlier manual recording already past its window, as it would any workflow's meetings.

**Progress (2026-11-12): code shipped, measurements outstanding.**

- **Item 1 (preflight).** `MeetingRecorder.start` reads free space through `RecordingWriteFailure.freeSpace` (REC19's smaller-of-two reading, so no second reader), on a GCD thread bounded at 2 s, after creating the directory and before any file of the recording exists. `RecordingLowSpace.preflight` answers in "covered" seconds: further recording that fits after reserving the merge (64,000 B/s of what is recorded) and one minute's growth, at 576,000 + 64,000 B/s. Under two hours it never refuses; at the end of a successful start it emits `recorder` / `recording_low_space_at_start` (`free_bytes`, `hours_covered`), and the session sets the HUD's state label to a countdown ("Disk: 1h 25m left") and posts one notification naming the free space and the time.
- **Item 2 (watch).** `LowSpaceWatch`, armed at the end of a successful start and stood down in `standDownTimersAndHooks` (every stop and the termination flush), re-reads every 60 s on a utility-queue `DispatchSourceTimer` and hops to main with a generation check (REC25's shape). `RecordingLowSpace.Policy` is the pure three-band decision: label under two hours, warn once under 15 minutes (`recording_low_space`), stop once when covered reaches zero (`recording_stopped_low_space`). The stop is `MeetingSessionController.handleLowSpace` -> `forceStop(reason: "low_space")` -> `stopRecording`, the user's Stop path, which merges; it never touches `onWriteFailure`.
- **Item 3a.** `readMonoFloat32` reads 480,000-frame blocks through one reused buffer into a mono array allocated once. Bit-identical to the whole-file read (`FluidAudioRunnerTests`, 16 kHz stereo s16 with a short last block, a 44.1 kHz file through the unchanged resample path, mono). The non-16 kHz path still hands the whole source-rate mono array to `resampleMono`, so it keeps that array plus the resampler's buffers at its peak, without the stereo buffer. The test also found that one whole-file `read(into:)` can come back short (49,152 of 50,001 frames on a mono Float32 WAV); the loop reads to the header's length (`daemon/docs/gotchas.md`).
- **Item 3 (instrumentation).** `TranscriptionMemoryProbe` brackets every `FluidAudioRunner.transcribe`: `phys_footprint` from `task_info(TASK_VM_INFO)` at the start, sampled at 4 Hz for the peak, and at the end, as one `transcription` / `transcription_memory` row (`phase: "job"`, `audio_sec`, `peak_fraction` of physical memory, the kernel's lifetime peak), plus one `phase: "idle"` row 15 minutes after the last job for the resident-model question.
- **Item 4.** Preferences ▸ Storage gains "Growth, last 30 days" with the projection sentence (`LibraryGrowth`, every top-level file named for a stem dated in the window, less a recording's `.mic.wav` / `.system.wav` / `.merging.wav`, so a pane opened mid-meeting does not count 2 GB an hour of working files). `mp doctor` warns under 30 days (`check_library_growth`, same definition in `mp.storage.library_growth`). `AudioRetention.decide` takes `defaultWorkflowID`: a candidate with no `workflow_id` follows the default's policy, a deleted workflow still keeps, and a sidecar that exists but cannot be read is skipped (SEC25), so it cannot fall through to the default. `RecordingSpaceBudget` holds both rates; the Storage caption, the workflow editor footer and the Obsidian sublabel read `finalPerHourText`, and every "0.7 GB" (Workflow.swift, the two Preferences strings, the editor footer, README, GUIDE, the doctor docstring, the JSX mockup) now says about 230 MB.

Decisions not in the spec: the stop band holds back one check interval of growth (60 s x 640,000 B/s, about 38 MB) on top of the merge, since a reading a minute late would otherwise land after "just enough"; a start already under 15 minutes is not warned again on the first tick; the HUD shows the countdown in the existing state label, with `HUDStateLabel` resolving the four label owners in one place (lost mic, then low disk, then off the record, then confirming end), rather than a new HUD element, so the pill's two shapes are unchanged. Not verified on screen: the daemon's HUD cannot be screenshotted here, so the label's look is the owner's to eyeball.
